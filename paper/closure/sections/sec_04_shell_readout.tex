\section{The shell, its residual and its closure}
\label{sec:shell-readout}

This section contains the mathematical core of the paper.  We first set
up the object that carries the two sides of the BSD formula and the
recognition hypothesis placed on it (\Cref{sec:shell-readout-basic}).
We then show that the Strong BSD identity is exactly a fixed-point
statement for an explicit closure operator (\Cref{sec:scalar-closure}),
and explain why sensitivity of the formula to its factors does not make
any hypothesis necessary (\Cref{sec:sensitivity}).

\subsection{The shell and the recognition predicate}
\label{sec:shell-readout-basic}

\begin{definition}[BSD trace shell]\label{def:closure:sel-bsd-shell}
A \emph{BSD trace shell} $\SelBSD$ consists of the following data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A curve $E$ and a natural number $r$.  In the intended application
  $E$ is an elliptic curve over $\Q$ and $r$ its analytic rank; the
  definition itself treats $E$ as a label.
\item A set $k$ of scalars with an element $0$ and operations $-$,
  $\cdot$ and $/$ satisfying the two laws
  \[
    a-b=0\iff a=b,
    \qquad
    (a/d)\cdot b=(a\cdot b)/d ,
  \]
  and elements of $k$ standing for the leading coefficient
  $L^*=L^{(r)}(E,1)/r!$, the order $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$, the regulator
  $\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$, the period $\Omega_E$, the Tamagawa product
  $\Tam(E)$ and the squared torsion order $d=\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$.
\item The arithmetic side and the residual
  \[
    R_E=\frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}{d},
    \qquad
    \rhoE=L^*-R_E .
  \]
\item Declared local scopes: a set $\mathcal L$ of local extensions, a
  \emph{descent scope} given by two predicates, one on natural numbers $n$
  (``$n$ is an additive bad prime in scope'') and one on pairs $(n,L)$
  with $L\in\mathcal L$ (``$L$ is a trivializing lift at $n$ in scope''),
  and a \emph{signed scope} given by a predicate on natural numbers.
\item A set $\mathcal F$, the \emph{formed layer}, with an idempotent map
  $C:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$, together with data called the duality
  datum $J^{!}_{\mathrm{BSD}}$, the visible readout
  $\mathrm{Vis}^{!}_{\mathrm{BSD}}$ and the audit record
  $\mathrm{Audit}^{!}_{\mathrm{BSD}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For an actual curve one takes $k=\R$ with its usual operations, finite
$\Sha$, and the BSD normalizations of the period and regulator; then
$\rhoE=0$ is the Strong BSD identity.  The definition keeps the
operations abstract so that the formal statements can be checked on
other scalar sets as well, for instance on $\Q$ in the control examples
of \Cref{sec:landing}.  The data in item~(5) are carried by the shell but
play no role in the scalar argument; the map $C$ is used only in
\Cref{thm:closure:formed-closure-bridge}.  The formalization also attaches
to the shell several propositions with supplied proofs (admissibility,
lawfulness, applicability and similar bookkeeping); nothing below depends
on them.

The recognition predicate collects the hypotheses of
\Cref{sec:recognition-sources} for the curve of the shell.

\begin{definition}[Recognition predicate $\PiBSD$]\label{def:closure:pi-bsd}
For a shell $\SelBSD$ with curve $E$, the predicate $\PiBSD(E)$ consists
of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every natural number $n$ and every $L\in\mathcal L$ such that
  $n$ and $(n,L)$ satisfy the two predicates of the descent scope, a
  $\GammaPD$ record at $(E,n,L)$ whose factor $\mathrm{tam}_n$ equals
  $\Tam(E)$;
\item for every natural number $n$ in the signed scope, a $\GammaSP$
  record at $(E,n)$ whose factor $\mathrm{sha}_n$ equals
  $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$;
\item if $r\ge2$, a $\GammaGZ$ record at $E$, of the same analytic rank,
  whose leading coefficient, regulator, period and Tamagawa factor are
  those of the shell, and whose constant is
  $\kapr(E)=\#\Sha(E/\Q)/d$;
\item if $r\in\{0,1\}$, the identity $L^*=R_E$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Item~(4) deserves a comment.  In rank $0$ and $1$ the predicate simply
contains the scalar Strong BSD identity as a hypothesis.  For many curves
and primes the $p$-part of that identity is known, through
Gross--Zagier, Kolyvagin and Iwasawa main
conjectures~\citep{GrossZagier1986,Kolyvagin1989,SkinnerUrban2014}
(\Cref{sec:t-cascade}), but the full identity is not known in general,
and nothing in this paper establishes it.  The predicate also records the local hypotheses, together with the
matching of local factors with the global ones.  The descent requirement
is vacuous when no pair $(n,L)$ satisfies both of its predicates, and the
signed requirement is vacuous when its predicate is empty.  If a
requirement is not vacuous, the records it demands exist only at primes
(odd primes, for descent), since every $\GammaPD$ and $\GammaSP$ record
is at a prime; in particular, if the descent scope admits a pair $(n,L)$
with $n$ not an odd prime, or the signed scope contains an $n$ that is not
prime, then $\PiBSD(E)$ cannot hold.

\begin{theorem}[Residual and Strong BSD]\label{thm:closure:pi-bsd-iff-strong-bsd}
For every shell, $\rhoE=0$ if and only if
\[
  \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
  =
  \frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}
       {\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}} .
\]
This equivalence concerns the two scalars only.  On the abstract shells
of \Cref{def:closure:sel-bsd-shell} there is no general construction of
$\PiBSD(E)$ from the identity.%
\footnote{Lean proves the equivalence for every shell.  The second
statement is checked on a symbolic shell, with rational scalars and a
label in place of a curve, on which the identity holds but whose declared
descent scope contains the index $2$, which no $\GammaPD$ record admits.
This is a statement about the abstract shell type, not an example of an
elliptic curve.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition $\rhoE=L^*-R_E$, and by the first law of the shell
$L^*-R_E=0$ if and only if $L^*=R_E$.  For the last assertion,
$\PiBSD$ demands records that the scalar identity does not provide; the
symbolic shell described in the footnote satisfies the identity but not
$\PiBSD$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strong BSD as a fixed-point statement}
\label{sec:scalar-closure}

In the Six Birds framework~\citep{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026} a
\emph{closure} on a set is an idempotent self-map, and an element is
\emph{closed} if the map fixes it.  The Strong BSD identity has an exact
formulation of this kind, with an explicit and elementary closure.

Let $k$ be a field with $2\neq0$, for example $\R$.  On $k\times k$ let
$J(a,b)=(b,a)$ be the swap and let
\[
  c=\tfrac12(\mathrm{Id}+J),\qquad c(a,b)=\Bigl(\frac{a+b}{2},\frac{a+b}{2}\Bigr),
\]
the average of a pair with its swap.  For a shell whose scalar set is
$k$, put $x_E=(L^*,R_E)$, the pair of the two sides of the BSD formula.

\begin{theorem}[Strong BSD as a fixed point]\label{thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item $c\circ c=c$; $c$ preserves the sum $a+b$; $c\circ J=c$ and
  $J\circ c=c$.  Moreover $c(x)$ is the only pair with equal coordinates
  and the same sum as $x$.
\item For every $x=(a,b)$: $c(x)=x$ if and only if $a=b$, if and only if
  $a-b=0$.
\item For every shell whose scalar set is the field $k$,
  \[
    c(x_E)=x_E
    \iff
    L^*=R_E
    \iff
    \rhoE=0 .
  \]
\item If $\PiBSD(E)$ holds, then $c(x_E)=x_E$.\footnote{Lean proves (1)--(4) for every field with $2\neq0$.  In (3) the
field structure need not agree with the operations of the shell: the
last equivalence uses the shell's own law $a-b=0\iff a=b$.  Statement (4)
combines (3) with \Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) Both coordinates of $c(x)$ equal $(a+b)/2$, so applying $c$ again
changes nothing, the sum is $a+b$, and swapping the input or the output
changes nothing.  A pair $(y,y)$ with $2y=a+b$ has $y=(a+b)/2$.
(2) If $c(x)=x$ then $a=(a+b)/2=b$; conversely if $a=b$ then
$(a+b)/2=a$.  (3) is (2) applied to $x_E$, followed by
\Cref{thm:closure:pi-bsd-iff-strong-bsd}.  (4) By
\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}, $\PiBSD(E)$ implies $L^*=R_E$;
apply (3).
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns the \emph{original} pair $x_E$.  It must not be
confused with the trivial fact that $c(x)$ is always closed: for
$x=(2,1)$ one has $c(x)=(3/2,3/2)$, which is closed, while $x$ itself is
not.  Replacing an unequal pair by its average proves nothing about the
pair.  Equivalently, asking that $c$ preserve the analytic coordinate,
$c(x)_1=x_1$, is already the same as asking that $x$ be closed.
\Cref{fig:averaging-closure} shows the geometry, and the
characteristic assumption is needed: if $2=0$ the average is not
defined.

The closure $c$ acts on two numbers.  The Six Birds formulation of a
problem asks for a closure on a richer \emph{formed layer} $\mathcal F$
whose readout is the scalar pair; for BSD this would be a layer of
Selmer, determinant and height data.  We have not constructed such a
layer.  What can be proved is what such a layer would have to satisfy in
order to return the scalar statement.

\begin{theorem}[Closure on a formed layer]\label{thm:closure:formed-closure-bridge}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a set with an idempotent map $C:\mathcal F\to\mathcal
F$, and let $q:\mathcal F\to k\times k$ be a readout that intertwines $C$
with $c$:
\[
  q(C(x))=c(q(x))\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal F .
\]
Write $q(x)=(q_1(x),q_2(x))$.  Then for every $x\in\mathcal F$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $q(C(x))=q(x)$ if and only if $q_1(x)=q_2(x)$;
\item if $C(x)=x$, then $q_1(x)=q_2(x)$;
\item if $q$ is injective, then $C(x)=x$ if and only if $q_1(x)=q_2(x)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for a shell with formed layer $\mathcal F$ and closure $C$,
an intertwining readout $q$ and a state $x$ with $q(x)=x_E$: if
$C(x)=x$ then the Strong BSD identity holds; and if $\PiBSD(E)$ holds then
$q(C(x))=q(x)$.%
\footnote{Lean proves all statements, with the intertwining identity, the
fixedness of the original state and the match $q(x)=x_E$ as explicit
hypotheses.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By intertwining, $q(C(x))=c(q(x))$, so (1) is
\Cref{thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation}(2) applied to $q(x)$.  If
$C(x)=x$ then $q(C(x))=q(x)$, and (1) gives (2).  If $q$ is injective,
$q(C(x))=q(x)$ implies $C(x)=x$, which with (2) gives (3).  For the shell,
(2) with $q(x)=x_E$ gives $L^*=R_E$; and $\PiBSD(E)$ gives $L^*=R_E$ by
\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}, so (1) gives $q(C(x))=q(x)$.
\end{proof}

Three small examples over $\Q$ show what the injectivity, fixedness and
intertwining hypotheses each contribute.  Let
$\mathcal F=(\Q\times\Q)\times\Q$, $C((a,b),t)=(c(a,b),0)$ and
$q((a,b),t)=(a,b)$; then $C$ is idempotent and $q$ intertwines.  The state
$((1,1),1)$ has equal readout but is not fixed by $C$, so without
injectivity the converse of (2) fails.  The state $((2,1),0)$ is not
fixed, its readout is unequal, and its image $C((2,1),0)=((3/2,3/2),0)$
is fixed: closing a state does not make the original state satisfy the
identity.  Finally the identity map of $\Q\times\Q$, with $q$ the
identity, fixes $(2,1)$ but does not intertwine with $c$: fixedness
without the intertwining identity gives nothing.

So the full closure formulation of Strong BSD rests on two inputs that
this paper does not supply: a formed layer with a readout intertwining its
closure with $c$, and the fixedness of the original state.  Idempotence
of a closure on its own forces neither.

\subsection{Sensitivity is not necessity}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

The arithmetic side $R_E$ depends on each of its factors: changing one
factor while keeping the others changes $R_E$.  This is elementary, and
we record it because it is easy to over-interpret.

\begin{theorem}[Factor sensitivity]\label{thm:closure:composite-signature}
Suppose the scalar set is a field, the multiplication and division of
the shell are the field operations, and $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$,
$\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$, $\Omega_E$, $\Tam(E)$ and $d$ are all nonzero.
If any one of $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$, $\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$ or $\Tam(E)$ in the
formula for $R_E$ is replaced by a different value, the other factors
being kept, the value of $R_E$ changes.%
\footnote{The formalization records this statement as a conditional
schema: the corresponding declaration takes a package of cancellation
laws as a hypothesis, and also returns several supplied propositions (for
instance, statements named computability and falsifiability) that carry
no proof of what their names suggest and that we do not use.  Separately,
Lean proves that, when the shell's multiplication and division are the
field operations, the cancellation package holds exactly when the five
displayed factors are nonzero.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In a field, multiplication by a nonzero element and division by $d\neq0$
are injective.  If, say, $\#\Sha$ is replaced by $s'\neq\#\Sha$, then
$s'\Reg\,\Omega\Tam/d\neq\#\Sha\Reg\,\Omega\Tam/d$ because
$\Reg\,\Omega\Tam/d\neq0$.  The other two cases are the same.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sensitivity does not make a hypothesis necessary]\label{rmk:closure:sensitivity-not-necessity}
One might hope to argue from \Cref{thm:closure:composite-signature} that
each recognition hypothesis is needed, since each ``governs'' a factor.
The argument fails.  In rank at least $2$, the hypothesis $\GammaGZ$ with
its normalization already implies $L^*=R_E$
(\Cref{sec:recognition-sources}), so deleting $\GammaPD$ and $\GammaSP$
does not affect the scalar conclusion.  Sensitivity of the product to its
factors says nothing about which hypotheses are needed to prove an
identity between the product and another number.

A genuine independence statement needs an interface in which the factors
are introduced before they are normalized.  Suppose native factors $a$ and
$b$ satisfy
\[
  N:\ L^*=a\,\Reg\,\Omega\,b,\qquad
  C_b:\ b=\Tam(E),\qquad
  C_a:\ a=\#\Sha(E/\Q)/d .
\]
Then $N$, $C_a$ and $C_b$ together imply $L^*=R_E$, and none of the three
can be dropped: with $\Reg=\Omega=\#\Sha=d=\Tam=1$, the triples
$(L^*,a,b)=(2,1,2)$, $(2,2,1)$ and $(2,1,1)$ satisfy exactly two of the
three premises and violate the identity, since $R_E=1\neq2=L^*$.  But
this independence is a property of the premises, not of the identity:
when $\Reg\,\Omega\neq0$ and $N$ holds, $L^*=R_E$ is equivalent to the
single equation $ab=(\#\Sha/d)\Tam(E)$, which can hold while both $C_a$
and $C_b$ fail (take $a=2$, $b=1/2$ and all other values $1$).  With
all values positive, the solutions are exactly $a=u\,\#\Sha/d$,
$b=\Tam(E)/u$ for $u>0$.  Lean checks these countermodels and the
classification over $\Q$.  None of these rational models is an elliptic
curve, and we draw no arithmetic conclusion from them.
\end{remark}
