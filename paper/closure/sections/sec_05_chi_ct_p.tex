\section{The Cassels--Tate comparison input}
\label{sec:chi-ct-p}

The input $\chiCTp$ of \Cref{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import} is meant to
read the $p$-primary part of $\Sha$ through a Pfaffian, the square root of
a determinant, of the Cassels--Tate
pairing~\citep{Cassels1962,PoonenStoll1999}.  We state what the input
gives, and then show on an actual curve why its selected generator is
genuinely additional data.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Cassels--Tate comparison]\label{thm:closure:chi-ct-p-comparison}
Suppose the input $\chiCTp$ holds at $(E,p)$.  Then $p$ is prime, the
hypotheses of item~(1) of \Cref{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import} hold, and
there is a map
\[
  \Pf_{\mathrm{Nek},p}^{\mathrm{or},\sqrt{\ }}:
  \sqrt{\Fitt}_{\mathrm{CT}}
    \bigl(H^1(C_p(E))/\mathrm{div},\,U_{2,2}\bigr)
  \longrightarrow
  \mathrm{vctp}_p
\]
with the canonical property, sending the selected generator
$\sqrt{F}_E^p$ to the selected element
$h_p^{\mathrm{CT}}(E)$, with normalized sign
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{NekCT}}=+1$.  Here the source is the square-root Fitting
object of the quotient by the divisible part of $H^1$ of the Selmer
complex $C_p(E)$, for the pairing induced by Nekov\'a\v{r}'s cup product
$U_{2,2}$.%
\footnote{The formalization records this as a conditional schema: the
map, the image of the generator and the sign are fields of the input, and
the conclusion is their projection.  The data compared are fixed for the
curve and prime before the input is asserted, and an input selecting a
different generator or target at the same $(E,p)$ is rejected.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each assertion is a component of the input.
\end{proof}

The theorem is as strong as its input and no stronger.  In particular, it
does not derive the map, the image or the sign from the background results
listed in \Cref{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import}, and the main theorem
(\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}) does not use it.  Two further
points, visible in the example below, concern the formulation itself.
First, the published comparison of Nekov\'a\v{r} concerns torsion classes
in $H^2$ of a Selmer complex, with a minus sign; a source written in terms
of $H^1/\mathrm{div}$ requires a shift of degree, which also inverts the
determinant line.  Second, the determinant (Fitting) ideal does not
determine a selected generator, and in particular does not determine
whether a prescribed generator has a square root.

\begin{example}[An ordinary Selmer complex at $p=3$]\label{ex:closure:1913b1}
Let $E$ be the curve $1913b1$, $y^2+xy=x^3+x^2-34x-135$, and $p=3$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Arithmetic data.}  $E$ has discriminant $-1913^2$, reduction
  of type $I_2$ at $1913$ with $c_{1913}=2$, rational torsion of order
  $2$, and good ordinary reduction at $3$ with $a_3=2$ and
  $\#\widetilde E(\mathbb F_3)=2$, so $3$ is not anomalous and divides none of the torsion order, the
  Tamagawa number and the order of the reduced group
  $\widetilde E(\mathbb F_3)$.  An exact modular-symbol computation gives
  $|L(E,1)|/\Omega_E=9/2\neq0$; the scale of the modular symbol is fixed
  by the optimality of $E$~\citep[Table~1]{Miller2011}, the semistable
  Manin-constant theorem~\citep{Cesnavicius2018} and the structure of the
  real period lattice.  By Gross--Zagier and
  Kolyvagin~\citep{GrossZagier1986,Kolyvagin1989}, $E(\Q)$ has rank $0$
  and $\Sha(E/\Q)$ is finite.  The
  three-descent and the upper bound reported in the proof of
  \citet[Theorem~7.2]{Miller2011} give $\Sha(E/\Q)[3^\infty]\cong(\Z/3\Z)^2$.
\item \emph{The Selmer complex.}  Let $C$ be the ordinary compact Selmer
  complex of $T_3(E)$ over $\Z_3$, with unramified conditions away from
  $3$.  The hypotheses of Macias Castillo--Sano~\citep{MaciasCastilloSano2026}
  needed to describe its cohomology hold; in particular the image of
  Frobenius at $5$, with characteristic polynomial $X^2-2X+5\equiv
  X^2-2X+2\pmod 3$, generates a nonsplit Cartan subgroup of
  $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)$.  One finds $H^1(C)=0$ and
  $H^2(C)\cong\Sha(E/\Q)[3^\infty]^{\vee}\cong(\Z/3\Z)^2$, and $C$ is
  quasi-isomorphic to the complex $\Z_3^2\xrightarrow{\,3J\,}\Z_3^2$ in
  degrees $1$ and $2$, with
  $J=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
\item \emph{The determinant ideal and two generators.}  Under the
  canonical trivialization of the acyclic complex over $\Q_3$, the inverse
  determinant line of $C$ has image the ideal $(9)\subset\Z_3$, the
  Fitting ideal of $H^2(C)$; the square-root ideal is $(3)$.  The elements
  $9$ and $18$ both generate $(9)$, since $2\in\Z_3^\times$.  But $9=3^2$
  is a square in $\Q_3$, while $18$ is not: a square root would have
  valuation $1$, and dividing it by $3$ would give a unit whose square is
  $2$ modulo $3$, which is impossible.  So every invariant that factors
  through the ideal, including its valuation, takes the same value on the
  two generators, while only one of them lies in the image of the fixed
  squaring map.
\item \emph{Stark bases.}  Using the theorems of
  Sakamoto~\citep{Sakamoto2018} and Macias Castillo--Sano on core
  vertices, one obtains Stark bases over $\Z/27\Z$, and compatible bases
  at all levels, with identical Fitting ideals at every level and
  different square-root behaviour of the prescribed basis.
\item \emph{Degree.}  Since $H^1(C)=0$ here, the quotient
  $H^1(C)/\mathrm{div}$ appearing in the source of
  \Cref{thm:closure:chi-ct-p-comparison} is zero; the finite group sits in
  $H^2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

The arithmetic steps in items (1), (2) and (4) are written arguments that
rely on the external results cited there; the descent and the upper bound
are published computations that we did not rerun.  Lean checks the
finite algebra: the presentation by $3J$ and its cokernel of order $9$,
the normalization of every perfect alternating form on $(\Z/3\Z)^2$, the
Cartan computation for every matrix over $\mathbb F_3$ with the given
trace and determinant, and the square-class argument in item~(3).  The
example does not construct the generator, target or orientation that the
input $\chiCTp$ prescribes.  Nor does it show that no square root can be
normalized from the ideal: the generator $3$ of the root ideal $(3)$ is
determined by the ideal and the trivialization.  What it shows is that
the square-root behaviour of a basis prescribed independently of the ideal
is not determined by the ideal; the comparison of the prescribed
generator, target and orientation with arithmetic objects remains part of
the input.
