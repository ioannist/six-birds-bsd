\section{Scope and non-claims}\label{sec:scope}
% statements-of-record rows resolved here: none

The following statements delimit what the paper establishes.  They are
summarized in \Cref{tab:nonclaims}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Not a proof of BSD.}  The main theorem derives the Strong BSD
  identity for a curve from a recognition hypothesis whose scalar part
  contains the identity in rank at most one and expresses it through a
  normalized leading-term identity in rank at least two.  It is a
  conditional theorem and an exact translation, not an independent
  derivation, and it makes no claim about minimal hypotheses.
\item \emph{The hypotheses are not derived.}  The recognition hypotheses
  and the comparison inputs are assumed.  The paper does not prove them,
  and does not derive the map, generator image or sign of the
  Cassels--Tate input from the background results it lists.  The example
  of \Cref{sec:chi-ct-p} checks the hypotheses of some of those results
  for one curve and prime, but the identification of the prescribed
  generator, target and orientation remains part of the input.
\item \emph{No necessity.}  We do not claim that any recognition
  hypothesis is necessary for the scalar conclusion.  In rank at least
  $2$, the normalized hypothesis $\GammaGZ$ already implies it, and
  sensitivity of the BSD product to its factors
  (\Cref{thm:closure:composite-signature}) is not necessity
  (\Cref{rmk:closure:sensitivity-not-necessity}).
\item \emph{No arithmetic formed layer is constructed.}  The closure formulation
  (\Cref{sec:scalar-closure}) returns the identity from a closure on a
  richer layer only under an intertwining readout and fixedness of the
  original state.  Apart from small rational models, no such layer is
  constructed; for arithmetic data these are assumptions.  No application of
  a general master theorem of the framework is claimed.
\item \emph{No computability claims.}  The formal shell carries
  propositions named computability and falsifiability with supplied
  proofs; they are not results.  The scalar identity can be checked when
  both sides are supplied in an effective representation with decidable
  equality, for example over $\Q$, but the paper provides no procedure for
  computing them or for deciding the recognition predicate.
\item \emph{Open inputs.}  We know of no theorem supplying the
  leading-term identity in rank at least $2$ or main conjectures with
  explicit local factors at additive primes $2$ and $3$
  (\Cref{rmk:closure:t-e12-gap,rmk:closure:t-bad-gap}).  The local
  hypotheses are stated in restricted scopes: the descent hypothesis
  excludes $p=2$, and no signed comparison at an additive prime $2$ is
  established.
\item \emph{The $\eta$-formula is not established.}  It is recorded only
  as a conditional certificate; its derivation from the cited results is
  not claimed, two of those results do not apply as stated, and no
  numerical value is offered as evidence (\Cref{sec:eta-formula}).
\item \emph{The register is bookkeeping.}  The refinement-stable closure
  of the audit register (\Cref{sec:aor-instance}) concerns the
  register's statuses under a declared refinement relation; it proves no
  arithmetic statement and does not formalize the general AOR theory.
\end{enumerate}
