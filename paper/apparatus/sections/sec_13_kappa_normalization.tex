\section[The normalization constant kappa-r]{The normalization constant $\kapr$}
\label{sec:kappa-normalization}

Some formulations of the leading term, including the one used in the
companion paper, write the arithmetic side as
$\kapr(E)\,\Omega_E\Reg_r(E)\,c_E$ with an unspecified constant
$\kapr(E)$.  This section identifies the constant for which that form is
the standard Strong BSD formula, and makes the hypotheses of that
identification explicit.  It is a comparison of two ways of writing the
same conjectural identity, not a derivation of either.

Throughout, $\Sha(E/\Q)$ is assumed finite, $\Omega_E$ is the BSD real
period, $\Reg_r(E)$ is the regulator ($1$ in rank $0$), $\Tam(E)=\prod_\ell
c_\ell(E)$, and
\[
  C=\Omega_E\,\Reg_r(E)\,\Tam(E),
  \qquad
  d=\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Normalization of $\kapr$]\label{thm:apparatus:kappa-r-normalization-equivalence}
Fix the convention $c_E=\Tam(E)$ and work in a field containing all the
quantities above (for curves, $\R$).  Assume $C\neq0$ and $d\neq0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The right-hand side of the cascade form
  \[
    \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}=\kapr(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Reg_r(E)\,c_E
  \]
  equals the right-hand side of the standard Strong BSD form
  \[
    \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
    =\frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_r(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}{d}
  \]
  if and only if
  \[
    \kapr(E)=\frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)}{\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}}
    \qquad\Bigl(\text{equivalently, } \kapr(E)\,d=\#\Sha(E/\Q)\Bigr).
  \]
\item If this normalization holds, the two forms are equivalent for every
  value of the left-hand side.  Conversely, if the two forms are
  equivalent for every value of the left-hand side, the normalization
  holds.
\end{enumerate}
For actual curves the hypotheses hold: $\Omega_E>0$, the regulator is
positive because the N\'eron--Tate pairing is positive definite on
$E(\Q)\otimes\R$, $\Tam(E)$ is a positive integer, and $d\ge1$.%
\footnote{The declaration recorded for this theorem is Lean's cancellation
of a nonzero common factor over the integers.  Additional Lean
declarations prove (1) and (2) over an arbitrary field, including the
reordering of the standard product and the cross-multiplied form, which
requires $d\neq0$.  Positivity of the period and the regulator for actual
curves is not formalized.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The standard right-hand side equals $(\#\Sha/d)\,C$, by commutativity and
the definition of division.  The cascade right-hand side is
$\kapr(E)\,C$.  Since $C\neq0$, $\kapr(E)\,C=(\#\Sha/d)\,C$ if and only if
$\kapr(E)=\#\Sha/d$; since $d\neq0$, this holds if and only if
$\kapr(E)\,d=\#\Sha$.  This proves (1).  For (2), equal right-hand sides
make the two equations equivalent for every left-hand side.  Conversely,
evaluate both forms at the left-hand side $\kapr(E)\,C$: the cascade form
holds there, so the standard form does too, and (1) gives the
normalization.
\end{proof}

Part (2) needs the quantifier over all values of the left-hand side.
Equivalence of the two equations at a single, fixed value says nothing:
for instance the equations $0=1$ and $0=2$ are both false, hence
logically equivalent, although $1\neq2$.  When we say below that the
cascade form ``agrees'' with the standard form we mean that their
right-hand sides are equal, as in (1).

The conditional closure of the companion paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDClosure2026}
uses this normalization to read its rank-$\ge2$ hypothesis as the
standard Strong BSD formula.
