\begin{abstract}
We formulate the Strong Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer formula for an elliptic
curve $E/\Q$ as a fixed-point statement and prove a conditional theorem
in that formulation.  Let $L^*$ be the leading Taylor coefficient of
$L(E,s)$ at $s=1$ and $R_E$ the arithmetic side of the formula.  We show
that the Strong BSD identity holds exactly when the pair $(L^*,R_E)$ is
fixed by the averaging closure $c(a,b)=((a+b)/2,(a+b)/2)$, and we
determine what a closure on a richer layer of arithmetic data would need
in order to return this statement: a readout intertwining it with $c$,
and fixedness of the original state.  The main theorem derives the
identity from a recognition hypothesis, which in rank at most one
contains the identity and in rank at least two is a leading-term
identity with the normalization
$\kappa_r=\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}^2$.  This hypothesis has the
strength of the conclusion, so the theorem is a conditional translation
of Strong BSD into the closure framework, not an independent derivation.
The other inputs, local descent and signed hypotheses and comparison
statements from the literature, are carried with their precise scope
but are not used for the scalar identity.  We show by examples what these
inputs contain beyond coarser data: for $121a1$ and $121b1$ the local
congruences cannot see the Tamagawa numbers $1$ and $2$, and for $1913b1$
at $p=3$ two generators of the same Selmer-complex determinant ideal
differ in whether they have a square root.  We also show that sensitivity
of the BSD product to its factors does not make any hypothesis necessary,
and we record a proposed overconvergent $\eta$-formula only as a
conditional certificate, explaining why the published results it was
modelled on do not apply as stated.  The paper does not prove BSD.  The
scalar algebra and the closure statements are proved in Lean~4; the
remaining statements are labelled by the exact extent of their
formalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}


For an elliptic curve $E/\Q$ of analytic rank $r$, the Strong
Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer formula~\citep{BirchSwinnertonDyer1965,Tate1966bsd}
asserts
\[
  \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
  =
  \frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}
       {\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}} .
\]
Known results control parts of this formula: Gross--Zagier and
Kolyvagin~\citep{GrossZagier1986,Kolyvagin1989} in rank at most one,
Iwasawa main conjectures~\citep{SkinnerUrban2014} for the $p$-parts at
many primes, and Cassels--Tate and Selmer-complex
methods~\citep{Flach1990,Nekovar2006} for the finite arithmetic.  They do
not give the whole formula in general.

This paper studies the formula from the point of view of the Six Birds
framework~\citep{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026,TsiokosAOR2026}, in which a
statement is formulated as the fixed point of a \emph{closure} (an
idempotent self-map), the deep inputs are named as \emph{recognition
hypotheses}, and an audit register records how each input is accounted
for.  No knowledge of the framework is needed: all definitions are given
here.  We prove three things.

\paragraph{A fixed-point formulation.}
Write $L^*$ for the left side and $R_E$ for the right side of the formula,
and let $c(a,b)=\bigl(\frac{a+b}2,\frac{a+b}2\bigr)$ be the average of a
pair and its swap.  Then $c$ is idempotent, and the Strong BSD identity
holds exactly when the pair $(L^*,R_E)$ is fixed by $c$
(\Cref{thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation}).  The statement concerns
the original pair: $c$ always produces a fixed pair, and replacing an
unequal pair by its average proves nothing.  We also determine what a
closure on a richer layer of arithmetic data would need in order to
return the identity: a readout intertwining it with $c$, and fixedness of
the original state (\Cref{thm:closure:formed-closure-bridge}).  We do not
construct such a layer.

\paragraph{A conditional theorem.}
The recognition predicate $\PiBSD(E)$ (\Cref{def:closure:pi-bsd})
collects three recognition hypotheses: a local $p$-adic descent
hypothesis $\GammaPD$, a signed local hypothesis $\GammaSP$ for the
$\Sha$ factor, and a leading-term hypothesis $\GammaGZ$ in rank at least
two; in rank zero and one it contains the scalar identity itself.  The
main theorem (\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}) states that
$\PiBSD(E)$, together with comparison inputs from the literature, implies
the Strong BSD identity for $E$.

We stress the strength of this hypothesis.  In rank at least two,
$\GammaGZ$ asserts $L^*=\kapr\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}\Omega_E\Tam(E)$, and with
the normalization $\kapr=\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ of the companion
paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDApparatus2026} this is the identity rewritten.  The
scalar conclusion follows from this rank-split part of the hypothesis by
explicit algebra; the local hypotheses and the comparison inputs are
carried but not used.  The theorem is thus a conditional translation of
Strong BSD into the closure framework, with every input named, and not
an independent derivation from weaker statements.

\paragraph{An accounting of the inputs.}
For each input we state exactly what is assumed and what its scope is,
and we give examples showing what the inputs contain beyond coarser data.
The local hypotheses are congruences modulo $p$-adic units, which cannot
see a Tamagawa number prime to $p$; for $121a1$ and $121b1$ at $11$ the
Tamagawa numbers are $1$ and $2$ (\Cref{sec:recognition-sources}).  The
Cassels--Tate input prescribes a generator of a square-root determinant
object; for the curve $1913b1$ at $3$, two generators of the same
determinant ideal differ in whether they have a square root
(\Cref{ex:closure:1913b1}).  A proposed overconvergent $\eta$-formula is
recorded only as a conditional certificate: its eigenvalue has slope
$1/2$ rather than the critical slope, and a cited theorem does not apply
at the curves where the formula was proposed (\Cref{sec:eta-formula}).
Finally, sensitivity of the BSD product to each factor does not make any
hypothesis necessary (\Cref{rmk:closure:sensitivity-not-necessity}).

\paragraph{Organization.}
\Cref{sec:imports} describes the comparison inputs and
\Cref{sec:recognition-sources} the recognition hypotheses.
\Cref{sec:shell-readout} contains the core: the shell carrying the two
sides of the formula, the recognition predicate, the fixed-point
formulation and the discussion of sensitivity.
\Cref{sec:chi-ct-p,sec:eta-formula,sec:t-cascade} discuss three further
inputs (the Cassels--Tate comparison, the $\eta$-formula, and the $p$-part
of the identity in rank at most one).  None of the theorems of these
sections is used to derive the scalar conclusion of the main theorem; the
Cassels--Tate input itself remains a carried hypothesis of that theorem.  \Cref{sec:obstructions} records the inputs that, to our
knowledge, no current theorem supplies.  \Cref{sec:landing} proves the
main theorem, \Cref{sec:aor-instance} sets up the audit register, and
\Cref{sec:scope} lists what the paper does not claim.  Many statements
are checked in Lean~4 without external mathematical libraries; each
footnote says whether Lean proves the statement, a narrower version, or
only records it as a conditional schema, and \Cref{app:formalization}
gives the details.
 \section{Imported comparison inputs}
\label{sec:imports}

The argument of this paper uses two kinds of hypotheses.  This section
describes the first kind: comparison statements from arithmetic geometry
that are taken as inputs.  The next section describes the second kind,
the recognition hypotheses on which the main theorem actually rests.  We
are explicit about what each input contains, because several of them are
assembled from published results whose hypotheses or normalizations
differ from what the input asserts.

Each input is attached to a fixed curve: the data it compares (a map, a
generator, a target, a pairing) are specified as a family indexed by the
curve, and possibly a prime, before the input is asserted at the curve
$E$ of the main theorem.  This prevents an input proved for one curve
from being used for another.

\begin{definition}[Cassels--Tate comparison input]\label{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import}
Fix $E/\Q$ and a prime $p$, together with: a square-root Fitting object
attached to the $p$-primary Cassels--Tate data of $E$, a target line
$\mathrm{vctp}_p$, a selected generator $\sqrt{F}_E^p$ of the former and a
selected element $h_p^{\mathrm{CT}}(E)$ of the latter, and a property of
maps between them called \emph{canonical}.  The Cassels--Tate comparison
input $\chiCTp$ at $(E,p)$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the hypotheses that $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]$ is finite, that the
  Cassels--Tate pairing is nondegenerate, that the Selmer-complex
  hypotheses of Sakamoto~\citep{Sakamoto2018} and Macias
  Castillo--Sano~\citep{MaciasCastilloSano2026} hold, and that
  $p\nmid\Tam(E)$, with $p$ prime;
\item a map with the canonical property, sending the selected generator
  to the selected target element;
\item a normalized sign $\Sigma_{\mathrm{NekCT}}=+1$;
\item eight propositions $T_{E1},\dots,T_{E8}$ recording background
  results from the theory of Stark systems and Selmer
  complexes~\citep{Sakamoto2018,MaciasCastilloSano2026}, determinant
  functors~\citep{KnudsenMumford1976}, and the comparison of pairings on
  Selmer complexes with the Cassels--Tate
  pairing~\citep{Nekovar2006,Flach1990,FisherSchaeferStoll2010}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The map, the image of the generator and the sign in items (2) and (3) are
part of the input; they are not derived from the background results in
item (4), and no statement-by-statement assignment of $T_{E1},\dots,T_{E8}$
to published theorems is fixed here.  The published results differ from
the input in several specific ways.  Nekov\'a\v{r}'s comparison
\citep[Proposition~10.8.7]{Nekovar2006} identifies the cup product on
torsion classes in $H^2$ of a Selmer complex with \emph{minus} the Flach
pairing~\citep{Flach1990}, so a sign correction and an identification of
degrees are needed.  The derived-height comparison of Macias
Castillo--Sano~\citep[Theorem~4.2]{MaciasCastilloSano2026} assumes an odd
prime of good ordinary reduction, and its relation between the
Bertolini--Darmon and Nekov\'a\v{r} pairings also carries a minus sign.
Sakamoto's theory~\citep{Sakamoto2018} works at odd primes.  The theorem of
Fisher--Schaefer--Stoll~\citep[Theorem~6.3]{FisherSchaeferStoll2010}
normalizes the classical Cassels--Tate pairing on $n$-Selmer times
$2$-Selmer; it is not a Fitting-ideal statement and does not supply an
odd-primary generator.  \Cref{sec:chi-ct-p} gives an arithmetic example
showing that the determinant ideal does not determine the selected
generator.

\begin{definition}[Selmer-complex pairing input]\label{def:closure:a-e-import}
Fix, for each curve, a target category, a Selmer complex, a pairing
target and a pairing.  The input $\AofE$ at $E$ asserts that, for $E$,
the Selmer complex of Nekov\'a\v{r}~\citep{Nekovar2006} carries its
intrinsic Cassels--Tate pairing, that the determinant-line formulation of
the leading-term conjecture in the sense of
Burns--Flach~\citep{BurnsFlach2001}, which builds on the work of
Bloch--Kato~\citep{BlochKato1990} and
Fontaine--Perrin-Riou~\citep{FontainePerrinRiou1994}, is compatible with
this Selmer complex and pairing for $E$ (related determinant methods
appear in \citep{BurnsMaciasCastilloSeo2024,MaciasCastilloSano2026}); this
compatibility is part of the hypothesis.  It further asserts that the pairing is bilinear (or categorical, in the
categorical formulation).  The notation $\AofE$ is local to this paper and
is unrelated to the analytic leading coefficient $A_E$ of
\citet{TsiokosBSDApparatus2026}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Archimedean comparison input in rank at least two]\label{def:closure:beilinson-import}
Fix, for each curve, a rank, an archimedean regulator, a determinant line,
an $L$-value side and a map from the regulator to the determinant line.
The input at $E$ asserts that the rank of $E$ is at least $2$ and that the
determinant--$L$-value comparison of Beilinson~\citep{Beilinson1985} holds
for $E$.  It is required only when the analytic rank is at least $2$, and
its rank must equal the analytic rank of $E$.
\end{definition}

These three inputs appear among the hypotheses of the main theorem
(\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}) because they describe the
arithmetic setting in which its recognition hypothesis is meant to be
interpreted.  As we will see, the scalar conclusion of that theorem is
derived from the recognition hypothesis alone; the inputs are carried
alongside it and returned, not used.

\begin{nonclaim}\label{nonclaim:closure:imports-not-derived}
The comparison inputs are hypotheses.  This paper does not prove them,
and does not derive the map, generator image or sign of $\chiCTp$ from
the cited background results.  The example of \Cref{sec:chi-ct-p} checks
the hypotheses of some of those results for one curve and prime, but the
prescribed generator, target, comparison map and orientation remain
supplied inputs.  In the formalization they are
structures whose fields are the asserted statements; they introduce no
axioms.
\end{nonclaim}
 \section{The recognition hypotheses}
\label{sec:recognition-sources}


In the Six Birds framework a \emph{recognition source} is arithmetic
content that a construction rests on but does not derive: it is stated
precisely, attached to the object, and used as a hypothesis.  In this
paper there are three, one for each of three factors of the BSD formula
that the formal construction does not supply by itself.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\GammaPD$ concerns the local Tamagawa factor at an odd prime of
  additive reduction;
\item $\GammaSP$ concerns the $\Sha$ factor, through a signed local
  comparison;
\item $\GammaGZ$ concerns the leading term in analytic rank at least $2$.
\end{itemize}
\Cref{tab:recognition-sources} summarizes them.  We say at once what will
matter for the main theorem: its scalar conclusion uses only $\GammaGZ$ in
rank at least $2$, and in rank at most $1$ the identity itself,
which the predicate contains as a hypothesis
(\Cref{sec:shell-readout}).  The two local hypotheses are part of the
recognition predicate, with their scopes recorded below, but the scalar
argument does not use them.

\begin{definition}[$\GammaPD$: $p$-adic descent hypothesis]\label{def:closure:gamma-padic-descent}
Let $E/\Q$ and let $p$ be an odd prime at which $E$ has additive
reduction of Kodaira type II or III, with $c_p(E)\in\{1,2\}$, with
$\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]=0$, and let $L/\Qp$ be a finite tamely ramified
extension over which $E$ acquires good reduction (equivalently, over
which inertia acts trivially on the $\ell$-adic Tate module for every
prime $\ell\neq p$; reduction of type II or III is potentially good).  The hypothesis
$\GammaPD$ at $(E,p,L)$ supplies a descent map
\[
  \mathrm{descent}_p^{\mathrm{OC}}:
  LC_{\mathrm{triv}}(L_p^{\mathrm{OC}}(E_L))
  \longrightarrow
  LC_{\mathrm{triv}}(L_p^{\mathrm{OC}}(E))
\]
between trivialized leading coefficients of ordinary/anticyclotomic local
data over $L$ and over $\Qp$, a unit $\varepsilon_p(E,L)\in\Zp^\times$, and
the congruence
\[
  LC_{\mathrm{triv}}(L_p^{\mathrm{OC}}(E)) \equiv
  c_p(E)^{-1}\,\varepsilon_p(E,L)\,
  \mathrm{descent}_p^{\mathrm{OC}}
    \bigl(LC_{\mathrm{triv}}(L_p^{\mathrm{OC}}(E_L))\bigr)
  \pmod{\Zp^\times}.
\]
It also carries a factor $\mathrm{tam}_p$, which the recognition predicate
of \Cref{sec:shell-readout} matches with the Tamagawa product of $E$.
\end{definition}

Two features of this hypothesis should be noted.  First, since $p$ is odd
and $c_p(E)\in\{1,2\}$, the number $c_p(E)$ is a $p$-adic unit (in
particular $p\nmid c_p(E)$).  The congruence modulo $\Zp^\times$ therefore
holds or fails independently of whether $c_p(E)$ is $1$ or $2$: it carries
no information about $c_p(E)$.  For example, the curves $121a1$ and
$121b1$ have additive reduction at $11$ of types II and III, with $c_{11}$
equal to $1$ and $2$ respectively, and these two values have the same
class modulo $\Z_{11}^\times$.  The value of $c_p(E)$ is recorded in the
hypothesis separately.  Second, the matching of a local factor with the
global Tamagawa product is itself a comparison.  On the curve
$y^2=x^3+385x+1225$, the two places $5$ and $7$ of type III each have
$c_p=2$, and the global Tamagawa product is $4$; a single local factor is
not the global one.

\begin{definition}[$\GammaSP$: signed $\Sha$-persistence hypothesis]\label{def:closure:gamma-sha-persistence}
Let $E/\Q$, let $p$ be a prime of additive reduction at which the signed
Selmer theory used here applies, and let $L/\Qp$ be a finite tamely
ramified extension over which $E$ acquires good reduction.  Let
$\mathrm{sp}_p(E,L)$ be the signed local pairing on the cover side,
$\cores_{L/\Qp}$ the corestriction from the cover-side target to a
base-side target, and
\[
  C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{III}}(E,p)
  =
  \det\bigl(\cores_{L/\Qp}\circ \mathrm{sp}_p(E,L)\bigr)
\]
the determinant of the composite.  This construction, its
determinant-line convention and its applicability at $(E,p,L)$ are part
of the hypothesis; the signed theory of Kobayashi and
Pollack~\citep{Kobayashi2003,Pollack2003}, developed for good
supersingular reduction, is background for it, not a source of it.  The
hypothesis $\GammaSP$ at $(E,p,L)$ asserts
\[
  C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{III}}(E,p)
  \equiv c_p(E)\pmod{\Zp^\times},
\]
and carries a factor $\mathrm{sha}_p$, which the recognition predicate
matches with $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$.
\end{definition}

The same remark applies: when $p\nmid c_p(E)$, the congruence does not
see the value of $c_p(E)$.  The determinant of a composite of a map into
the cover-side target with corestriction to the base side requires a
determinant-line convention, which is part of the hypothesis, as is the
matching of $\mathrm{sha}_p$ with the global order of $\Sha$.

\begin{definition}[$\GammaGZ$: leading-term hypothesis in rank at least two]\label{def:closure:gamma-higher-gz-fixity}
Let $E/\Q$ be without complex multiplication, of analytic rank $r\ge2$.
Put
\[
  \psi_-(E,r)
  =
  \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
  -
  \kapr(E)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E).
\]
The hypothesis $\GammaGZ$ at $E$ asserts $\psi_-(E,r)=0$.  In the
recognition predicate it is used together with the normalization
$\kapr(E)=\#\Sha(E/\Q)/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ and with the matching of
its regulator, period and Tamagawa factors to those of $E$.
\end{definition}

The name refers to the kind of theorem that would be expected to supply
this hypothesis, a generalized Gross--Zagier formula in rank at least
$2$.  No theorem supplying this normalized higher-rank identity is
established among the inputs used here, and we retain it as a hypothesis
(see also \Cref{rmk:closure:t-e12-gap}).  It is
important to be clear about the strength of $\GammaGZ$.  Assume
$\Sha(E/\Q)$ finite, and let $\Omega_E$ and $\Tam(E)=\prod_vc_v(E)$ be as
in the BSD formula.  By the normalization of $\kapr$
\citep[Section~13]{TsiokosBSDApparatus2026}, the arithmetic term of
$\psi_-(E,r)$ is then
\[
  \kapr(E)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)
  =
  \frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}
       {\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}},
\]
so $\GammaGZ$, read with this normalization, is the Strong BSD identity for
$E$.  The hypothesis is not weaker than the conclusion it is used for.

\begin{nonclaim}\label{nonclaim:closure:recognition-sources-not-derived}
None of the three recognition hypotheses is derived in this paper, from
the Six Birds framework or otherwise.  Each is a hypothesis about a given
curve, recorded in the formalization as a structure whose arithmetic
content is a field, not as an axiom.  We also do not claim that any of
them is necessary for the scalar conclusion: in rank at least $2$,
$\GammaGZ$ with its normalization already implies it.
\end{nonclaim}
 \section{The shell, its residual and its closure}
\label{sec:shell-readout}

This section contains the mathematical core of the paper.  We first set
up the object that carries the two sides of the BSD formula and the
recognition hypothesis placed on it (\Cref{sec:shell-readout-basic}).
We then show that the Strong BSD identity is exactly a fixed-point
statement for an explicit closure operator (\Cref{sec:scalar-closure}),
and explain why sensitivity of the formula to its factors does not make
any hypothesis necessary (\Cref{sec:sensitivity}).

\subsection{The shell and the recognition predicate}
\label{sec:shell-readout-basic}

\begin{definition}[BSD trace shell]\label{def:closure:sel-bsd-shell}
A \emph{BSD trace shell} $\SelBSD$ consists of the following data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A curve $E$ and a natural number $r$.  In the intended application
  $E$ is an elliptic curve over $\Q$ and $r$ its analytic rank; the
  definition itself treats $E$ as a label.
\item A set $k$ of scalars with an element $0$ and operations $-$,
  $\cdot$ and $/$ satisfying the two laws
  \[
    a-b=0\iff a=b,
    \qquad
    (a/d)\cdot b=(a\cdot b)/d ,
  \]
  and elements of $k$ standing for the leading coefficient
  $L^*=L^{(r)}(E,1)/r!$, the order $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$, the regulator
  $\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$, the period $\Omega_E$, the Tamagawa product
  $\Tam(E)$ and the squared torsion order $d=\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$.
\item The arithmetic side and the residual
  \[
    R_E=\frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}{d},
    \qquad
    \rhoE=L^*-R_E .
  \]
\item Declared local scopes: a set $\mathcal L$ of local extensions, a
  \emph{descent scope} given by two predicates, one on natural numbers $n$
  (``$n$ is an additive bad prime in scope'') and one on pairs $(n,L)$
  with $L\in\mathcal L$ (``$L$ is a trivializing lift at $n$ in scope''),
  and a \emph{signed scope} given by a predicate on natural numbers.
\item A set $\mathcal F$, the \emph{formed layer}, with an idempotent map
  $C:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$, together with data called the duality
  datum $J^{!}_{\mathrm{BSD}}$, the visible readout
  $\mathrm{Vis}^{!}_{\mathrm{BSD}}$ and the audit record
  $\mathrm{Audit}^{!}_{\mathrm{BSD}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For an actual curve one takes $k=\R$ with its usual operations, finite
$\Sha$, and the BSD normalizations of the period and regulator; then
$\rhoE=0$ is the Strong BSD identity.  The definition keeps the
operations abstract so that the formal statements can be checked on
other scalar sets as well, for instance on $\Q$ in the control examples
of \Cref{sec:landing}.  The data in item~(5) are carried by the shell but
play no role in the scalar argument; the map $C$ is used only in
\Cref{thm:closure:formed-closure-bridge}.  The formalization also attaches
to the shell several propositions with supplied proofs (admissibility,
lawfulness, applicability and similar bookkeeping); nothing below depends
on them.

The recognition predicate collects the hypotheses of
\Cref{sec:recognition-sources} for the curve of the shell.

\begin{definition}[Recognition predicate $\PiBSD$]\label{def:closure:pi-bsd}
For a shell $\SelBSD$ with curve $E$, the predicate $\PiBSD(E)$ consists
of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every natural number $n$ and every $L\in\mathcal L$ such that
  $n$ and $(n,L)$ satisfy the two predicates of the descent scope, a
  $\GammaPD$ record at $(E,n,L)$ whose factor $\mathrm{tam}_n$ equals
  $\Tam(E)$;
\item for every natural number $n$ in the signed scope, a $\GammaSP$
  record at $(E,n)$ whose factor $\mathrm{sha}_n$ equals
  $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$;
\item if $r\ge2$, a $\GammaGZ$ record at $E$, of the same analytic rank,
  whose leading coefficient, regulator, period and Tamagawa factor are
  those of the shell, and whose constant is
  $\kapr(E)=\#\Sha(E/\Q)/d$;
\item if $r\in\{0,1\}$, the identity $L^*=R_E$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Item~(4) deserves a comment.  In rank $0$ and $1$ the predicate simply
contains the scalar Strong BSD identity as a hypothesis.  For many curves
and primes the $p$-part of that identity is known, through
Gross--Zagier, Kolyvagin and Iwasawa main
conjectures~\citep{GrossZagier1986,Kolyvagin1989,SkinnerUrban2014}
(\Cref{sec:t-cascade}), but the full identity is not known in general,
and nothing in this paper establishes it.  The predicate also records the local hypotheses, together with the
matching of local factors with the global ones.  The descent requirement
is vacuous when no pair $(n,L)$ satisfies both of its predicates, and the
signed requirement is vacuous when its predicate is empty.  If a
requirement is not vacuous, the records it demands exist only at primes
(odd primes, for descent), since every $\GammaPD$ and $\GammaSP$ record
is at a prime; in particular, if the descent scope admits a pair $(n,L)$
with $n$ not an odd prime, or the signed scope contains an $n$ that is not
prime, then $\PiBSD(E)$ cannot hold.

\begin{theorem}[Residual and Strong BSD]\label{thm:closure:pi-bsd-iff-strong-bsd}
For every shell, $\rhoE=0$ if and only if
\[
  \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
  =
  \frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}
       {\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}} .
\]
This equivalence concerns the two scalars only.  On the abstract shells
of \Cref{def:closure:sel-bsd-shell} there is no general construction of
$\PiBSD(E)$ from the identity.\footnote{Lean proves the equivalence for every shell.  The second
statement is checked on a symbolic shell, with rational scalars and a
label in place of a curve, on which the identity holds but whose declared
descent scope contains the index $2$, which no $\GammaPD$ record admits.
This is a statement about the abstract shell type, not an example of an
elliptic curve.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition $\rhoE=L^*-R_E$, and by the first law of the shell
$L^*-R_E=0$ if and only if $L^*=R_E$.  For the last assertion,
$\PiBSD$ demands records that the scalar identity does not provide; the
symbolic shell described in the footnote satisfies the identity but not
$\PiBSD$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strong BSD as a fixed-point statement}
\label{sec:scalar-closure}

In the Six Birds framework~\citep{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026} a
\emph{closure} on a set is an idempotent self-map, and an element is
\emph{closed} if the map fixes it.  The Strong BSD identity has an exact
formulation of this kind, with an explicit and elementary closure.

Let $k$ be a field with $2\neq0$, for example $\R$.  On $k\times k$ let
$J(a,b)=(b,a)$ be the swap and let
\[
  c=\tfrac12(\mathrm{Id}+J),\qquad c(a,b)=\Bigl(\frac{a+b}{2},\frac{a+b}{2}\Bigr),
\]
the average of a pair with its swap.  For a shell whose scalar set is
$k$, put $x_E=(L^*,R_E)$, the pair of the two sides of the BSD formula.

\begin{theorem}[Strong BSD as a fixed point]\label{thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item $c\circ c=c$; $c$ preserves the sum $a+b$; $c\circ J=c$ and
  $J\circ c=c$.  Moreover $c(x)$ is the only pair with equal coordinates
  and the same sum as $x$.
\item For every $x=(a,b)$: $c(x)=x$ if and only if $a=b$, if and only if
  $a-b=0$.
\item For every shell whose scalar set is the field $k$,
  \[
    c(x_E)=x_E
    \iff
    L^*=R_E
    \iff
    \rhoE=0 .
  \]
\item If $\PiBSD(E)$ holds, then $c(x_E)=x_E$.\footnote{Lean proves (1)--(4) for every field with $2\neq0$.  In (3) the
field structure need not agree with the operations of the shell: the
last equivalence uses the shell's own law $a-b=0\iff a=b$.  Statement (4)
combines (3) with the scalar argument in the proof of
\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}, which uses only $\PiBSD(E)$.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) Both coordinates of $c(x)$ equal $(a+b)/2$, so applying $c$ again
changes nothing, the sum is $a+b$, and swapping the input or the output
changes nothing.  A pair $(y,y)$ with $2y=a+b$ has $y=(a+b)/2$.
(2) If $c(x)=x$ then $a=(a+b)/2=b$; conversely if $a=b$ then
$(a+b)/2=a$.  (3) is (2) applied to $x_E$, followed by
\Cref{thm:closure:pi-bsd-iff-strong-bsd}.  (4) By the scalar argument in the proof of
\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}, which uses only $\PiBSD(E)$,
$\PiBSD(E)$ implies $L^*=R_E$; apply (3).
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns the \emph{original} pair $x_E$.  It must not be
confused with the trivial fact that $c(x)$ is always closed: for
$x=(2,1)$ one has $c(x)=(3/2,3/2)$, which is closed, while $x$ itself is
not.  Replacing an unequal pair by its average proves nothing about the
pair.  Equivalently, asking that $c$ preserve the analytic coordinate,
$c(x)_1=x_1$, is already the same as asking that $x$ be closed.
\Cref{fig:averaging-closure} shows the geometry, and the
characteristic assumption is needed: if $2=0$ the average is not
defined.

The closure $c$ acts on two numbers.  The Six Birds formulation of a
problem asks for a closure on a richer \emph{formed layer} $\mathcal F$
whose readout is the scalar pair; for BSD this would be a layer of
Selmer, determinant and height data.  We have not constructed such a
layer.  What can be proved is what such a layer would have to satisfy in
order to return the scalar statement.

\begin{theorem}[Closure on a formed layer]\label{thm:closure:formed-closure-bridge}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a set with an idempotent map $C:\mathcal F\to\mathcal
F$, and let $q:\mathcal F\to k\times k$ be a readout that intertwines $C$
with $c$:
\[
  q(C(x))=c(q(x))\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal F .
\]
Write $q(x)=(q_1(x),q_2(x))$.  Then for every $x\in\mathcal F$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $q(C(x))=q(x)$ if and only if $q_1(x)=q_2(x)$;
\item if $C(x)=x$, then $q_1(x)=q_2(x)$;
\item if $q$ is injective, then $C(x)=x$ if and only if $q_1(x)=q_2(x)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for a shell with formed layer $\mathcal F$ and closure $C$,
an intertwining readout $q$ and a state $x$ with $q(x)=x_E$: if
$C(x)=x$ then the Strong BSD identity holds; and if $\PiBSD(E)$ holds then
$q(C(x))=q(x)$.\footnote{Lean proves all statements, with the intertwining identity, the
fixedness of the original state and the match $q(x)=x_E$ as explicit
hypotheses.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By intertwining, $q(C(x))=c(q(x))$, so (1) is
\Cref{thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation}(2) applied to $q(x)$.  If
$C(x)=x$ then $q(C(x))=q(x)$, and (1) gives (2).  If $q$ is injective,
$q(C(x))=q(x)$ implies $C(x)=x$, which with (2) gives (3).  For the shell,
(2) with $q(x)=x_E$ gives $L^*=R_E$; and $\PiBSD(E)$ gives $L^*=R_E$ by
the scalar argument in the proof of
\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}, so (1) gives $q(C(x))=q(x)$.
\end{proof}

Three small examples over $\Q$ show what the injectivity, fixedness and
intertwining hypotheses each contribute.  Let
$\mathcal F=(\Q\times\Q)\times\Q$, $C((a,b),t)=(c(a,b),0)$ and
$q((a,b),t)=(a,b)$; then $C$ is idempotent and $q$ intertwines.  The state
$((1,1),1)$ has equal readout but is not fixed by $C$, so without
injectivity the converse of (2) fails.  The state $((2,1),0)$ is not
fixed, its readout is unequal, and its image $C((2,1),0)=((3/2,3/2),0)$
is fixed: closing a state does not make the original state satisfy the
identity.  Finally the identity map of $\Q\times\Q$, with $q$ the
identity, fixes $(2,1)$ but does not intertwine with $c$: fixedness
without the intertwining identity gives nothing.

So the full closure formulation of Strong BSD rests on two inputs that
this paper does not supply: a formed layer with a readout intertwining its
closure with $c$, and the fixedness of the original state.  Idempotence
of a closure on its own forces neither.

\subsection{Sensitivity is not necessity}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

The arithmetic side $R_E$ depends on each of its factors: changing one
factor while keeping the others changes $R_E$.  This is elementary, and
we record it because it is easy to over-interpret.

\begin{theorem}[Factor sensitivity]\label{thm:closure:composite-signature}
Suppose the scalar set is a field, the multiplication and division of
the shell are the field operations, and $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$,
$\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$, $\Omega_E$, $\Tam(E)$ and $d$ are all nonzero.
If any one of $\#\Sha(E/\Q)$, $\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$ or $\Tam(E)$ in the
formula for $R_E$ is replaced by a different value, the other factors
being kept, the value of $R_E$ changes.\footnote{The formalization records this statement as a conditional
schema: the corresponding declaration takes a package of cancellation
laws as a hypothesis, and also returns several supplied propositions (for
instance, statements named computability and falsifiability) that carry
no proof of what their names suggest and that we do not use.  Separately,
Lean proves that, when the shell's multiplication and division are the
field operations, the cancellation package holds exactly when the five
displayed factors are nonzero.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In a field, multiplication by a nonzero element and division by $d\neq0$
are injective.  If, say, $\#\Sha$ is replaced by $s'\neq\#\Sha$, then
$s'\Reg\,\Omega\Tam/d\neq\#\Sha\Reg\,\Omega\Tam/d$ because
$\Reg\,\Omega\Tam/d\neq0$.  The other two cases are the same.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sensitivity does not make a hypothesis necessary]\label{rmk:closure:sensitivity-not-necessity}
One might hope to argue from \Cref{thm:closure:composite-signature} that
each recognition hypothesis is needed, since each ``governs'' a factor.
The argument fails.  In rank at least $2$, the hypothesis $\GammaGZ$ with
its normalization already implies $L^*=R_E$
(\Cref{sec:recognition-sources}), so deleting $\GammaPD$ and $\GammaSP$
does not affect the scalar conclusion.  Sensitivity of the product to its
factors says nothing about which hypotheses are needed to prove an
identity between the product and another number.

A genuine independence statement needs an interface in which the factors
are introduced before they are normalized.  Suppose native factors $a$ and
$b$ satisfy
\[
  N:\ L^*=a\,\Reg\,\Omega\,b,\qquad
  C_b:\ b=\Tam(E),\qquad
  C_a:\ a=\#\Sha(E/\Q)/d .
\]
Then $N$, $C_a$ and $C_b$ together imply $L^*=R_E$, and none of the three
can be dropped: with $\Reg=\Omega=\#\Sha=d=\Tam=1$, the triples
$(L^*,a,b)=(2,1,2)$, $(2,2,1)$ and $(2,1,1)$ satisfy exactly two of the
three premises and violate the identity, since $R_E=1\neq2=L^*$.  But
this independence is a property of the premises, not of the identity:
when $\Reg\,\Omega\neq0$ and $N$ holds, $L^*=R_E$ is equivalent to the
single equation $ab=(\#\Sha/d)\Tam(E)$, which can hold while both $C_a$
and $C_b$ fail (take $a=2$, $b=1/2$ and all other values $1$).  With
all values positive, the solutions are exactly $a=u\,\#\Sha/d$,
$b=\Tam(E)/u$ for $u>0$.  Lean checks these countermodels and the
classification over $\Q$.  None of these rational models is an elliptic
curve, and we draw no arithmetic conclusion from them.
\end{remark}
 \section{The Cassels--Tate comparison input}
\label{sec:chi-ct-p}

The input $\chiCTp$ of \Cref{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import} is meant to
read the $p$-primary part of $\Sha$ through a Pfaffian, the square root of
a determinant, of the Cassels--Tate
pairing~\citep{Cassels1962,PoonenStoll1999}.  We state what the input
gives, and then show on an actual curve why its selected generator is
genuinely additional data.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Cassels--Tate comparison]\label{thm:closure:chi-ct-p-comparison}
Suppose the input $\chiCTp$ holds at $(E,p)$.  Then $p$ is prime, the
hypotheses of item~(1) of \Cref{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import} hold, and
there is a map
\[
  \Pf_{\mathrm{Nek},p}^{\mathrm{or},\sqrt{\ }}:
  \sqrt{\Fitt}_{\mathrm{CT}}
    \bigl(H^1(C_p(E))/\mathrm{div},\,U_{2,2}\bigr)
  \longrightarrow
  \mathrm{vctp}_p
\]
with the canonical property, sending the selected generator
$\sqrt{F}_E^p$ to the selected element
$h_p^{\mathrm{CT}}(E)$, with normalized sign
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{NekCT}}=+1$.  Here the source is the square-root Fitting
object of the quotient by the divisible part of $H^1$ of the Selmer
complex $C_p(E)$, for the pairing induced by Nekov\'a\v{r}'s cup product
$U_{2,2}$.\footnote{The formalization records this as a conditional schema: the
map, the image of the generator and the sign are fields of the input, and
the conclusion is their projection.  The data compared are fixed for the
curve and prime before the input is asserted, and an input selecting a
different generator or target at the same $(E,p)$ is rejected.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each assertion is a component of the input.
\end{proof}

The theorem is as strong as its input and no stronger.  In particular, it
does not derive the map, the image or the sign from the background results
listed in \Cref{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import}, and the main theorem
(\Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}) does not use it.  Two further
points, visible in the example below, concern the formulation itself.
First, the published comparison of Nekov\'a\v{r} concerns torsion classes
in $H^2$ of a Selmer complex, with a minus sign; a source written in terms
of $H^1/\mathrm{div}$ requires a shift of degree, which also inverts the
determinant line.  Second, the determinant (Fitting) ideal does not
determine a selected generator, and in particular does not determine
whether a prescribed generator has a square root.

\begin{example}[An ordinary Selmer complex at $p=3$]\label{ex:closure:1913b1}
Let $E$ be the curve $1913b1$, $y^2+xy=x^3+x^2-34x-135$, and $p=3$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Arithmetic data.}  $E$ has discriminant $-1913^2$, reduction
  of type $I_2$ at $1913$ with $c_{1913}=2$, rational torsion of order
  $2$, and good ordinary reduction at $3$ with $a_3=2$ and
  $\#\widetilde E(\mathbb F_3)=2$, so $3$ is not anomalous and divides none of the torsion order, the
  Tamagawa number and the order of the reduced group
  $\widetilde E(\mathbb F_3)$.  An exact modular-symbol computation gives
  $|L(E,1)|/\Omega_E=9/2\neq0$; the scale of the modular symbol is fixed
  by the optimality of $E$~\citep[Table~1]{Miller2011}, the semistable
  Manin-constant theorem~\citep{Cesnavicius2018} and the structure of the
  real period lattice.  By Gross--Zagier and
  Kolyvagin~\citep{GrossZagier1986,Kolyvagin1989}, $E(\Q)$ has rank $0$
  and $\Sha(E/\Q)$ is finite.  The
  three-descent and the upper bound reported in the proof of
  \citet[Theorem~7.2]{Miller2011} give $\Sha(E/\Q)[3^\infty]\cong(\Z/3\Z)^2$.
\item \emph{The Selmer complex.}  Let $C$ be the ordinary compact Selmer
  complex of $T_3(E)$ over $\Z_3$, with unramified conditions away from
  $3$.  The hypotheses of Macias Castillo--Sano~\citep{MaciasCastilloSano2026}
  needed to describe its cohomology hold; in particular the image of
  Frobenius at $5$, with characteristic polynomial $X^2-2X+5\equiv
  X^2-2X+2\pmod 3$, generates a nonsplit Cartan subgroup of
  $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)$.  One finds $H^1(C)=0$ and
  $H^2(C)\cong\Sha(E/\Q)[3^\infty]^{\vee}\cong(\Z/3\Z)^2$, and $C$ is
  quasi-isomorphic to the complex $\Z_3^2\xrightarrow{\,3J\,}\Z_3^2$ in
  degrees $1$ and $2$, with
  $J=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
\item \emph{The determinant ideal and two generators.}  Under the
  canonical trivialization of the acyclic complex over $\Q_3$, the inverse
  determinant line of $C$ has image the ideal $(9)\subset\Z_3$, the
  Fitting ideal of $H^2(C)$; the square-root ideal is $(3)$.  The elements
  $9$ and $18$ both generate $(9)$, since $2\in\Z_3^\times$.  But $9=3^2$
  is a square in $\Q_3$, while $18$ is not: a square root would have
  valuation $1$, and dividing it by $3$ would give a unit whose square is
  $2$ modulo $3$, which is impossible.  So every invariant that factors
  through the ideal, including its valuation, takes the same value on the
  two generators, while only one of them lies in the image of the fixed
  squaring map.
\item \emph{Stark bases.}  Using the theorems of
  Sakamoto~\citep{Sakamoto2018} and Macias Castillo--Sano on core
  vertices, one obtains Stark bases over $\Z/27\Z$, and compatible bases
  at all levels, with identical Fitting ideals at every level and
  different square-root behaviour of the prescribed basis.
\item \emph{Degree.}  Since $H^1(C)=0$ here, the quotient
  $H^1(C)/\mathrm{div}$ appearing in the source of
  \Cref{thm:closure:chi-ct-p-comparison} is zero; the finite group sits in
  $H^2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

The arithmetic steps in items (1), (2) and (4) are written arguments that
rely on the external results cited there; the descent and the upper bound
are published computations that we did not rerun.  Lean checks the
finite algebra: the presentation by $3J$ and its cokernel of order $9$,
the normalization of every perfect alternating form on $(\Z/3\Z)^2$, the
Cartan computation for every matrix over $\mathbb F_3$ with the given
trace and determinant, and the square-class argument in item~(3).  The
example does not construct the generator, target or orientation that the
input $\chiCTp$ prescribes.  Nor does it show that no square root can be
normalized from the ideal: the generator $3$ of the root ideal $(3)$ is
determined by the ideal and the trivialization.  What it shows is that
the square-root behaviour of a basis prescribed independently of the ideal
is not determined by the ideal; the comparison of the prescribed
generator, target and orientation with arithmetic objects remains part of
the input.
 \section{The overconvergent \texorpdfstring{$\eta$}{eta}-formula}
\label{sec:eta-formula}

This section records a proposed $p$-adic identity at additive odd primes,
the overconvergent $\eta$-formula.  It plays no role in the main theorem.
We state it only as a conditional input, and we explain why the published
results one might hope to derive it from do not apply in the form needed.

\begin{definition}[$\eta$-certificate]\label{def:closure:eta-published-imports}
Let $E/\Q$ and let $p$ be an odd prime at which $E$ has additive reduction.
An \emph{$\eta$-certificate} at $(E,p)$ consists of: a nonzero $\beta$
(the proposed $U_p$-eigenvalue of an additive $p^2$-stabilization) with
$\beta^2=-p$; a leading term $T_{\mathrm{lead}}(E,p)$ of an overconvergent
expansion, with its order $\mathrm{ord}_T(E,p)\in\Z$; a value
$\etap(E)$; nonzero scalars standing for $p$ and $\#E(\Q)_{\tors}$; and
the identity
\[
  \etap(E)
  =
  \bigl(p\,\#E(\Q)_{\tors}\bigr)^{\mathrm{ord}_T(E,p)-1}
  \,\beta^{-2}\,
  T_{\mathrm{lead}}(E,p).
\]
The certificate also lists, as propositions, the background results it
is modelled on: Bella\"iche's critical-slope theory and moment
lemma~\citep{Bellaiche2012}, overconvergent modular
symbols~\citep{PollackStevens2011,ColemanMazur1998}, critical $p$-adic
$L$-functions~\citep{BenoisBuyukboduk2024}, the Eisenstein ideal at
prime-square level~\citep{LangWake2025}, component groups of N\'eron
models~\citep{Lorenzini1993,Ling1997}, and a nonvanishing and primitivity
statement for a cuspidal evaluation, which is a conjecture.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional $\eta$-formula]\label{thm:closure:oc-eta-formula}
If $(E,p)$ carries an $\eta$-certificate, then
\[
  \etap(E)
  =
  \bigl(p\,\#E(\Q)_{\tors}\bigr)^{\mathrm{ord}_T(E,p)-1}
  \,\beta^{-2}\,
  T_{\mathrm{lead}}(E,p).
\]\footnote{The formalization records this as a conditional schema: the
identity is a field of the certificate and the conclusion is its
projection.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity is part of the certificate.
\end{proof}

We do not claim that the listed background results, together with the
conjecture, imply the identity.  Two elementary obstructions show that at
least some of them do not apply as stated.

First, the relation $\beta^2=-p$ fixes the slope of $\beta$.  With the
valuation normalized by $v_p(p)=1$, it gives $2v_p(\beta)=1$, so
$v_p(\beta)=1/2$.  In weight $2$ the critical slope is $1$; this is the
normalization of Benois--B\"uy\"ukboduk~\citep{BenoisBuyukboduk2024}, for
whom the critical slope in weight $k$ is $k-1$.  So $\beta$ is not of
critical slope, and results about critical-slope eigenvalues do not apply
to it without a further comparison.

Second, the theorem of Lang--Wake~\citep[Theorem~1.1]{LangWake2025}
concerns a level prime $N$ and a residual prime $\ell$, both at least $5$,
with $\ell\mid N+1$; these numerical conditions are necessary, though not
sufficient, for an application.  They exclude the three curves at which
the formula has been proposed, whichever residual prime is chosen.  For
$36a1$ at $p=3$, the level prime would be $3<5$ (and the full level $36$
is not prime).  For $49a1$ at $p=7$, $N+1=8$ has no prime divisor at least
$5$.  For $121b1$ at $p=11$, $N+1=12$ has no prime divisor at least $5$.

Numerical values of $\etap$ at these three curves have been recorded,
but without complete input data or a reproducible computation of the
right-hand side.  We do not regard them as evidence for the formula, at
those curves or in general.\footnote{Lean proves the slope computation (from the relation
$\beta^2=-p$, the multiplicativity of the valuation at $\beta^2$ and
$v_p(-p)=1$, it derives $v_p(\beta)=1/2\neq1$) and the exclusion of the
level primes $3$, $7$ and $11$ from the numerical conditions of
Lang--Wake for every residual prime.}
 \section{The \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-part in rank at most one}
\label{sec:t-cascade}

In analytic rank $0$ and $1$, the recognition predicate contains the
scalar Strong BSD identity itself as a hypothesis
(\Cref{def:closure:pi-bsd}).  This section records, for context, the
form in which known results are expected to bear on that hypothesis: they
give the $p$-part of the identity for suitable primes $p$.  As with the
other inputs, we state precisely what is assumed; the main theorem does not
use this section.

\begin{definition}[Rank-at-most-one input]\label{def:closure:t-cascade-import}
The rank-at-most-one input is a list of forty propositions, each with an
asserted proof, standing for published results in two branches: for curves
with complex multiplication, Rubin's Iwasawa theory over imaginary
quadratic fields~\citep{Rubin1991}, the main conjecture at supersingular
primes of Pollack--Rubin~\citep[Theorem~7.3]{PollackRubin2004}, and work of
Burungale--Castella--Skinner--Tian~\citep{BurungaleCastellaSkinnerTian2022}
at good ordinary primes, together with the archimedean, Heegner-point and
uniformization ingredients needed to compare $p$-adic and classical
quantities; for curves without complex multiplication, the work of
Skinner--Urban~\citep{SkinnerUrban2014}, Wei Zhang~\citep{WeiZhang2014},
Castella et al.~\citep{CHKLL2025} and Yan--Zhu~\citep{YanZhu2026}.  Both
branches are part of the input simultaneously.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional $p$-part identity in rank at most one]\label{thm:closure:t-cascade-rank-le-1}
Let $E/\Q$ have analytic rank $r\in\{0,1\}$ and let $p$ be a prime.
Assume the hypotheses of one of the two branches:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(CM)] $E$ has complex multiplication by $K$; either $p$ is good
  ordinary and split in $K$, or $p\ge5$ is good supersingular and inert in
  $K$; $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]$ is finite, the Cassels--Tate pairing is
  nondegenerate, the Selmer-complex hypotheses of Sakamoto and Macias
  Castillo--Sano hold, $p\nmid\Tam(E)$, the standard ramification and
  Galois-image hypotheses hold, and a Heegner point is given when $r=1$;
\item[(non-CM)] $E$ has no complex multiplication, the residual
  representation $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ is surjective~\citep{Serre1972}, $p$ is
  good ordinary with $p\nmid N$ and $p\nmid\Tam(E)$; the local,
  residual-irreducibility, ramification and level hypotheses of
  Skinner--Urban hold; $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]$ is finite, the Cassels--Tate
  pairing is nondegenerate, the Selmer-complex hypotheses hold, and, when
  $r=1$, the Gross--Zagier--Kolyvagin hypotheses hold for a Heegner field.
\end{itemize}
Assume the rank-at-most-one input, and assume further that, under these
branch hypotheses, the results it lists yield the valuation identity
below.  Then
\[
  v_p\!\left(\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)/r!}{\Omega_E\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)}\right)
  =
  v_p\!\left(\frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Tam(E)}{\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}}\right).
\]\footnote{The formalization records this as a conditional schema.  Lean
proves that the comparison hypothesis, given the forty asserted
propositions and a curve and prime, is equivalent to the bare implication
from the branch hypotheses to the valuation identity; the theorem is
therefore exactly as strong as that implication.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the assumed implication to the branch hypotheses.
\end{proof}

The comparison hypothesis in the theorem is where the arithmetic is: in
the non-CM branch, for instance, one divisibility comes from the
Skinner--Urban main conjecture and the other from Euler systems and the
Gross--Zagier--Kolyvagin method, and making the two match up to $p$-adic
units requires the finiteness, nondegeneracy and nondivisibility
hypotheses.  We have not verified that the listed results yield the
identity under exactly the stated hypotheses, and the input is not
organized curve by curve.  The theorem should be read as a statement of
the expected form of the known $p$-part results, not as a new proof of
them, and it does not supply the full identity required by the
recognition predicate in rank at most one.
 \section{Inputs not supplied by known theorems}
\label{sec:obstructions}

Two of the inputs above correspond to well-known open problems.  We record
them as remarks rather than theorems: they describe what, to our
knowledge, is not currently available, and we have not carried out a
systematic search of the literature to support anything stronger.

\begin{remark}[Leading terms in rank at least two]\label{rmk:closure:t-e12-gap}
For $E/\Q$ without complex multiplication and of analytic rank $r\ge2$,
we know of no theorem giving an identity of the form
\[
  \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
  =
  \kapr(E)\,\det\bigl(\langle Z_i,Z_j\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}\bigr)\,
  \Omega_E\,\Tam(E)
\]
with explicit classes $Z_1,\dots,Z_r$ forming a $\Z$-basis of
$E(\Q)/E(\Q)_{\tors}$, so that the determinant is $\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$
(for points spanning a subgroup of index $m$ it would be
$m^2\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)$), and explicit normalizing factors, that is, a generalized Gross--Zagier formula in rank at least $2$.  The
hypothesis $\GammaGZ$ is exactly what such a theorem, combined with the
normalization of $\kapr$, would supply.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Main conjectures at additive primes $2$ and $3$]\label{rmk:closure:t-bad-gap}
For $E/\Q$ with additive reduction at $p\in\{2,3\}$, we know of no
Iwasawa main conjecture giving an equality of characteristic ideals with
explicit local correction factors.  This is to be distinguished from the
signed theories at primes of good supersingular reduction and from other
local constructions.  The hypothesis $\GammaPD$ excludes the prime $2$.  The
hypothesis $\GammaSP$ requires separately supplied signed applicability
and comparison data, and no such comparison at an additive prime $2$ is
established here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Where the local residuals are carried]\label{rmk:closure:mode-b-residuals}
In the bookkeeping of the Six Birds framework, the difficulties of the
previous two remarks and of the signed local comparison appear as three
residuals: a rank-$r$ leading-term residual, a signed corestriction
residual, and a local descent residual.  They are not additional results.
They are carried, respectively, by the hypotheses $\GammaGZ$, $\GammaSP$
and $\GammaPD$, and naming them does not reduce what has to be supplied.
\end{remark}

\label{sec:landing}

We can now state the main result.  \Cref{fig:landing-chain-diagram}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Strong BSD]\label{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional}
Let $\SelBSD$ be a shell with curve $E$ and analytic rank $r$, and let
$C$ be its closure operator.  Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the recognition predicate $\PiBSD(E)$
  (\Cref{def:closure:pi-bsd});
\item the Cassels--Tate input $\chiCTp$ at $E$ and some prime $p$, and the
  Selmer-complex pairing input $\AofE$ at $E$
  (\Cref{def:closure:chi-ct-p-import,def:closure:a-e-import});
\item if $r\ge2$, the archimedean comparison input at $E$, of rank $r$
  (\Cref{def:closure:beilinson-import}).
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
  \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
  =
  \frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_{\mathrm{NT}}(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}
       {\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}},
\]
equivalently $\rhoE=0$, and the asserted components of the inputs in
(2) and (3) hold.\footnote{The formalization records this theorem as a conditional
schema, because it returns the components of the inputs in (2) and (3)
by projection.  Its scalar conclusion, however, is derived: Lean proves
it from items (3) and (4) of $\PiBSD(E)$ by the algebra in the proof
below.  A compiled dependency check confirms that the two declarations
isolating this algebra depend on no project declaration outside their
own module,
and that neither the derivation from $\PiBSD(E)$ nor the full theorem
depends on \Cref{thm:closure:chi-ct-p-comparison},
\Cref{thm:closure:oc-eta-formula}, \Cref{thm:closure:composite-signature},
the statements of \Cref{sec:aor-instance}, or the supplied applicability,
computability, falsifiability and ablation fields of the shell.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $r\in\{0,1\}$, item (4) of $\PiBSD(E)$ is the identity $L^*=R_E$.
If $r\ge2$, item (3) supplies a $\GammaGZ$ record whose factors are those
of the shell and whose constant is $\kapr=\#\Sha/d$, with $d$ the squared
torsion order.  Its hypothesis $\psi_-(E,r)=0$ says
$L^*-\kapr\Reg\,\Omega\,\Tam=0$, hence $L^*=\kapr\Reg\,\Omega\,\Tam$ by the
first law of the shell.  Substituting $\kapr=\#\Sha/d$ and applying the
second law $(a/d)\cdot b=(a\cdot b)/d$ three times gives
\[
  L^*
  =\Bigl(\frac{\#\Sha}{d}\Bigr)\Reg\,\Omega\,\Tam
  =\frac{\#\Sha\,\Reg\,\Omega\,\Tam}{d}
  =R_E .
\]
In both cases $\rhoE=0$ by \Cref{thm:closure:pi-bsd-iff-strong-bsd}.  The
remaining assertions are components of the inputs.
\end{proof}

Three comments make the content of the theorem precise.

\emph{What the proof uses.}  The scalar conclusion is derived from the
rank-split part of $\PiBSD(E)$ alone.  The local hypotheses $\GammaPD$
and $\GammaSP$ and the comparison inputs are hypotheses of the theorem,
because they belong to the arithmetic setting in which the recognition
predicate is meant, but the scalar identity does not depend on them.

\emph{How strong the hypothesis is.}  The rank-split scalar premise
contains the identity in rank $0$ and $1$, and expresses it through
normalized fixity in rank at least $2$: $\GammaGZ$ with the normalization
$\kapr=\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ is the identity rewritten.  The
remaining hypotheses supply additional records, which the identity does
not reconstruct.  The theorem is therefore not an independent derivation of
Strong BSD from weaker arithmetic statements, and it is not a statement
about minimal hypotheses.  Its content is the exact translation: the
identity is equivalent to the vanishing of $\rhoE$
(\Cref{thm:closure:pi-bsd-iff-strong-bsd}) and, when the scalars form a
field with $2\neq0$, to the fixedness of $x_E$ under the averaging closure
(\Cref{thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation}),
and it follows from the recognition predicate by explicit algebra, with
every arithmetic input named and scoped.

\emph{That the hypotheses are consistent and not vacuous.}  The theorem
has been instantiated on shells with scalars in $\Q$, a label in place of
a curve, all factors equal to $1$, and rank $0$ or $2$.  With leading
coefficient $1$, all hypotheses hold and the conclusion follows; in rank
$0$ no archimedean input is required, and none can be supplied.  With
leading coefficient $2$ and everything else unchanged, the recognition
predicate fails, since it would force $2=1$.  These are formal checks of
the statement, not examples of elliptic curves.

\begin{nonclaim}\label{nonclaim:closure:landing-conditional}
This is not a proof of the Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, in its weak
or strong form, for any curve.  The theorem derives the Strong BSD
identity for $E$ from hypotheses whose scalar part contains it in rank at
most one and expresses it through normalized fixity in rank at least two.
We know of no theorem supplying the leading-term identity in rank at
least two, or the main conjectures at additive primes $2$ and $3$
(\Cref{rmk:closure:t-e12-gap,rmk:closure:t-bad-gap}).
\end{nonclaim}
 \section{The audit register}
\label{sec:aor-instance}

The Six Birds framework accompanies a closure argument with a register
that records, for each residual difficulty, how it has been accounted for:
constructed, imported from the literature, or supplied as a recognition
hypothesis.  Audited Operational Realisability (AOR)~\citep{TsiokosAOR2026} is the
framework's notion of such an accounting; its strongest form,
refinement-stable AOR, asks that the accounting survive refinement.  This
section sets up the register for the shell and proves a local model of
refinement-stable AOR for it, in a precise and limited sense.  The register is bookkeeping: it does not prove any
arithmetic statement, and an entry marked as supplied remains a
hypothesis.

\begin{definition}[Audit register of the shell]\label{def:closure:aor-sel-instance}
A \emph{discharge atom} consists of a residual type, a list of secondary
residual types, and a status: \emph{by construction}, \emph{approved
external input}, \emph{bridged} (supplied as a recognition hypothesis),
or one of a few other statuses, among them \emph{zero} (eliminated).  A
\emph{register} consists of an identifier, lists of observations, routes,
sources, interfaces, constraints and nonclaims (the last nonempty), and a
list of atoms.  The register of the shell $\SelBSD$, given recognition
records $\GammaPD$, $\GammaSP$, $\GammaGZ$ and inputs $\chiCTp$, $\AofE$
and the archimedean input, has eight atoms (\Cref{tab:aor-discharge-register}):
the construction of the shell and the normalization of $\kapr$, both by
construction; the three comparison inputs, as approved external inputs;
and the three recognition hypotheses, each bridged, with the secondary
types role, target, transport and limit.  Since the archimedean input
requires rank at least $2$, the register as set up applies to shells of
analytic rank at least $2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The constructed and imported entries]\label{thm:closure:aor-mechanical-records}
In the register of the shell, the construction of the shell and the
normalization of $\kapr$ have status by construction, and the three
comparison inputs have status approved external input.  Moreover, if the
quantities are integers or lie in a field (with the shell's multiplication
being the field multiplication) and $C=\Omega_E\Reg\,\Tam(E)\neq0$, then
one has $\kapr C=sC$ if and only if
$\kapr=s$, for every $s$ (for $s=\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ this is
the normalization of $\kapr$); and the asserted components of the three
inputs hold.\footnote{Lean proves the status assignments and the cancellation, the
latter for integer-valued quantities; the components of the inputs are
returned from the input records.  Lean does not show that the inputs are
approved in any sense beyond this label.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The statuses are read off the register.  The cancellation is
\citet[Section~13]{TsiokosBSDApparatus2026} in the integral case: if a
common factor is nonzero, two multiples of it agree exactly when the
multipliers agree.  The last assertion restates the inputs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The recognition entries]\label{thm:closure:aor-recognition-discharge}
In the register of the shell, each of the three recognition hypotheses is
an atom of residual type \emph{source}, with secondary types role,
target, transport and limit, and with status bridged; none has status
zero.  The asserted content of the three recognition records holds.\footnote{The formalization records this as a conditional schema: the
statuses are read off the register, and the asserted content is returned
from the records.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the definition of the register, and because the recognition records
are hypotheses.
\end{proof}

The register is \emph{closed} if its list of nonclaims is nonempty and no
atom has status zero.  A \emph{refinement} of a register keeps its
identifier and all entries of its lists, and replaces each atom by atoms
with the same residual type and the same status and with at least the
same secondary types, every new atom refining an old one.  Refinement in
this sense adds detail but cannot change a status; in particular it cannot
turn a bridged hypothesis into an eliminated one.

\begin{theorem}[Refinement-stable closure of the register]\label{thm:closure:aor-instance}
The register of the shell is closed, and every refinement of it is
closed.  The recognition entries satisfy
\Cref{thm:closure:aor-recognition-discharge}, and, when
$\Omega_E\Reg\,\Tam(E)\neq0$ in the setting of
\Cref{thm:closure:aor-mechanical-records}, so do the constructed and
imported entries.\footnote{Lean proves this for the refinement relation just described,
which it shows to be reflexive and transitive and to preserve statuses.
It does not formalize the general refinement relation of the AOR
framework.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The nonclaims list is nonempty and the eight statuses are by
construction, approved external input, or bridged, none of them zero.  A
refinement preserves the nonclaims and the status of every atom it
accounts for, so it is again closed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the register does and does not say]\label{rmk:closure:aor-partial-status}
Closedness of the register means that every residual difficulty has been
given a status other than ``eliminated''.  Three of them are bridged:
they are carried as recognition hypotheses, which this paper does not
prove.  The register therefore records an accounting of the conditional
theorem, not a proof of Strong BSD.  Its stability is proved only for the
declared refinement relation, and the label ``approved'' on an input is
not a verification of that input.
\end{remark}
 \section{Scope and non-claims}\label{sec:scope}


The following statements delimit what the paper establishes.  They are
summarized in \Cref{tab:nonclaims}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Not a proof of BSD.}  The main theorem derives the Strong BSD
  identity for a curve from a recognition hypothesis whose scalar part
  contains the identity in rank at most one and expresses it through a
  normalized leading-term identity in rank at least two.  It is a
  conditional theorem and an exact translation, not an independent
  derivation, and it makes no claim about minimal hypotheses.
\item \emph{The hypotheses are not derived.}  The recognition hypotheses
  and the comparison inputs are assumed.  The paper does not prove them,
  and does not derive the map, generator image or sign of the
  Cassels--Tate input from the background results it lists.  The example
  of \Cref{sec:chi-ct-p} checks the hypotheses of some of those results
  for one curve and prime, but the identification of the prescribed
  generator, target and orientation remains part of the input.
\item \emph{No necessity.}  We do not claim that any recognition
  hypothesis is necessary for the scalar conclusion.  In rank at least
  $2$, the normalized hypothesis $\GammaGZ$ already implies it, and
  sensitivity of the BSD product to its factors
  (\Cref{thm:closure:composite-signature}) is not necessity
  (\Cref{rmk:closure:sensitivity-not-necessity}).
\item \emph{No arithmetic formed layer is constructed.}  The closure formulation
  (\Cref{sec:scalar-closure}) returns the identity from a closure on a
  richer layer only under an intertwining readout and fixedness of the
  original state.  Apart from small rational models, no such layer is
  constructed; for arithmetic data these are assumptions.  No application of
  a general master theorem of the framework is claimed.
\item \emph{No computability claims.}  The formal shell carries
  propositions named computability and falsifiability with supplied
  proofs; they are not results.  The scalar identity can be checked when
  both sides are supplied in an effective representation with decidable
  equality, for example over $\Q$, but the paper provides no procedure for
  computing them or for deciding the recognition predicate.
\item \emph{Open inputs.}  We know of no theorem supplying the
  leading-term identity in rank at least $2$ or main conjectures with
  explicit local factors at additive primes $2$ and $3$
  (\Cref{rmk:closure:t-e12-gap,rmk:closure:t-bad-gap}).  The local
  hypotheses are stated in restricted scopes: the descent hypothesis
  excludes $p=2$, and no signed comparison at an additive prime $2$ is
  established.
\item \emph{The $\eta$-formula is not established.}  It is recorded only
  as a conditional certificate; its derivation from the cited results is
  not claimed, two of those results do not apply as stated, and no
  numerical value is offered as evidence (\Cref{sec:eta-formula}).
\item \emph{The register is bookkeeping.}  The refinement-stable closure
  of the audit register (\Cref{sec:aor-instance}) concerns the
  register's statuses under a declared refinement relation; it proves no
  arithmetic statement and does not formalize the general AOR theory.
\end{enumerate}

  \section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}


The classical results bearing on the Strong BSD formula each control part
of it.  Gross--Zagier and Kolyvagin~\citep{GrossZagier1986,Kolyvagin1989}
relate derivatives, Heegner points, Selmer groups and regulators in rank
at most one; Iwasawa main conjectures~\citep{SkinnerUrban2014} give
$p$-parts of the formula at many primes; Cassels--Tate pairings and
Selmer complexes~\citep{Flach1990,Nekovar2006} organize the finite
arithmetic.  None of them gives the whole formula in general.  This paper
does not change that.  What it does is make one precise statement about
how the formula fits a closure framework, and record exactly which inputs
such a formulation needs.

The precise statement is the fixed-point formulation of
\Cref{thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation}: the identity holds exactly
when the pair of its two sides is fixed by the averaging closure.  It is
elementary, and it is exact; in particular it separates fixedness of the
original pair from the trivial closedness of its image.
\Cref{thm:closure:formed-closure-bridge} says what a closure on a richer
layer of arithmetic data would have to satisfy to return the identity: a
readout intertwining it with the averaging closure, and fixedness of the
original state.  Neither is constructed here, and idempotence alone does
not supply them.

The main theorem should be read with its hypotheses in view.  Its scalar
conclusion follows from a recognition hypothesis that contains the
identity in rank at most one and expresses it through a normalized
leading-term identity in rank at least two.  The other hypotheses, the
local recognition hypotheses and the comparison inputs, are carried with
their scopes but do not enter the derivation.  The value of the
formulation therefore lies in its accounting: what is assumed, where each
assumption would come from, what its scope is, and what is not known.
Several points of that accounting are not obvious and are worth
recording.  The local congruences modulo $\Zp^\times$ cannot see a
Tamagawa number prime to $p$ (\Cref{sec:recognition-sources}).  The
determinant ideal of a Selmer complex does not determine whether a
prescribed generator has a square root (\Cref{ex:closure:1913b1}).  The
proposed $\eta$-formula involves an eigenvalue of slope $1/2$, not the
critical slope (\Cref{sec:eta-formula}).  And sensitivity of the BSD
product to its factors does not make any hypothesis necessary
(\Cref{rmk:closure:sensitivity-not-necessity}).

The companion paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDApparatus2026} studies the same
factors from the other side: which data each factor of the BSD formula
retains, with finite adequacy tests and examples of information loss.
Its normalization of $\kapr$ is what identifies the rank-$\ge2$
recognition hypothesis with the Strong BSD identity here.

The same pattern, a target statement written as the fixed point of a
closure with the deep inputs named as recognition hypotheses and an
audit register recording their status, is the general method of the Six
Birds framework~\citep{TsiokosFoundationsIII2026,TsiokosAOR2026}.  For BSD
it yields a conditional theorem of exactly the strength of its
hypotheses, together with a precise description of the inputs that an
unconditional proof along these lines would require.
 \section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}


We placed the two sides of the Strong BSD formula in a shell and showed
that the identity is equivalent to the vanishing of their difference and
to the fixedness of the pair under an explicit averaging closure.  We
proved that the identity follows from a rank-split recognition
hypothesis, which contains the identity in rank at most one and
expresses it through a normalized leading-term identity in rank at least
two; the theorem is therefore a conditional translation rather than an
independent derivation.  We stated each further input, the local
recognition hypotheses and the comparisons from the literature, with its
verified scope, and showed by examples where these inputs carry
information that coarser data do not: the square-root behaviour of a
prescribed Selmer-complex generator, and Tamagawa numbers invisible to
congruences modulo units.  The leading-term identity in rank at least two,
and main conjectures at additive primes $2$ and $3$, are inputs that, as
far as we know, no current theorem supplies.

\section{Lean formalization and supporting data}\label{app:formalization}

\subsection*{What is formalized}

The Lean~4 development accompanying this paper imports no external
mathematics library.  Its theorems depend only on Lean's standard axioms
(propositional extensionality, quotient soundness and classical choice,
together with their standard consequences); there are no project-specific
axioms, and no declaration is left unproved.  The comparison inputs and
the recognition hypotheses are Lean structures whose fields are the
asserted statements; they are hypotheses, not axioms.  None of the
arithmetic geometry of the paper is formalized.

Each of the $30$ labelled definitions, theorems, remarks and nonclaims
falls into one of five classes, used in the footnotes and in
\Cref{tab:formalization-coverage}.  (The example of \Cref{sec:chi-ct-p}
is not one of these statements.)
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Lean proves} (3 theorems):
  \Cref{thm:closure:pi-bsd-iff-strong-bsd,thm:closure:scalar-closure-translation,thm:closure:formed-closure-bridge}.
  The Lean statement has the content of the theorem in the body.
\item \emph{Model version} (2 theorems):
  \Cref{thm:closure:aor-mechanical-records,thm:closure:aor-instance},
  proved for integer quantities and for the declared refinement relation
  respectively.
\item \emph{Conditional schema} (6 theorems):
  \Cref{thm:closure:composite-signature,thm:closure:chi-ct-p-comparison,thm:closure:oc-eta-formula,thm:closure:t-cascade-rank-le-1,thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional,thm:closure:aor-recognition-discharge}.
  Part or all of the conclusion is obtained by projecting a supplied
  hypothesis.  For \Cref{thm:closure:strong-bsd-conditional} only the
  returned input components are projected: its scalar conclusion is
  derived, and the two declarations isolating that derivation depend on
  no project declaration outside their own module.  A compiled dependency check
  confirms this, and that the derivation does not depend on the auxiliary
  theorems of \Cref{sec:chi-ct-p,sec:eta-formula,sec:aor-instance}, on
  \Cref{thm:closure:composite-signature}, or on the supplied applicability,
  computability, falsifiability and ablation fields of the shell.
\item \emph{Lean definition} (11 definitions): the shell, the
  recognition predicate, the three recognition hypotheses, the five input
  records and the audit register.
\item \emph{Not formalized} (8 statements): five remarks and three
  nonclaims.
\end{itemize}
These classes follow the coverage records in the repository, which compare
each Lean statement with the corresponding statement of the paper.  The
main theorem and the fixed-point formulation have controls on rational
shells, including false targets that the recognition predicate rejects;
rational-carrier examples test the hypotheses of the formed-layer
statement (\Cref{sec:landing,sec:scalar-closure}).

\section{Version history}\label{app:version-history}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em, style=nextline]
\item[v1, 31 August 2026] First public version.
\item[v2, 3 October 2026] Revised after a statement-by-statement audit
  of the mathematics and of its Lean formalization.  Changes:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The main theorem is stated with the strength of its hypothesis
    made explicit: its scalar conclusion is derived from the rank-split
    part of the recognition predicate, and the other hypotheses are
    carried but not used.  The claims that a closure master theorem
    applies, that each recognition hypothesis is indispensable, and that
    the recognition predicate is computable and falsifiable are
    withdrawn; the factor-sensitivity statement is retained with a remark
    explaining why it does not imply necessity.
  \item New results: the fixed-point formulation of Strong BSD under the
    averaging closure, and the conditions under which a closure on a
    richer layer returns it.
  \item The comparison inputs are attached to the curve of the theorem,
    with the archimedean input required only in rank at least two; the
    Cassels--Tate input states its map, generator, target and sign as
    hypotheses, with the discrepancies from the cited literature
    described and an example on $1913b1$ at $p=3$ added.
  \item The local recognition hypotheses state their scopes; it is noted
    that their congruences modulo units do not see Tamagawa numbers prime
    to $p$, and that matching local and global factors is a further
    comparison.
  \item The $\eta$-formula is recorded only as a conditional certificate;
    its claimed derivation from published results and its numerical
    anchors are withdrawn, and two scope obstructions are given.
  \item The rank-at-most-one statement makes its comparison hypothesis
    explicit; the two literature no-go theorems become remarks; the audit
    register is described as a local model of refinement-stable AOR.
  \item Lean coverage is described statement by statement; the text is
    rewritten throughout for readability.
  \end{itemize}
