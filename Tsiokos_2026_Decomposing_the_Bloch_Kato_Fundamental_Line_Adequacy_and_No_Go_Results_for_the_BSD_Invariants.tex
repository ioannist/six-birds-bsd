\begin{abstract}
The Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer formula for an elliptic curve $E/\Q$ is a
product of pieces of different kinds: a period, a regulator, Tamagawa
numbers, torsion and the order of the Tate--Shafarevich group, which in
the Bloch--Kato formulation are joined with local $p$-adic data and
orientation conventions in one determinant line.  Each piece is the image
of richer data.  We separate these data into five columns
(height/regulator, analytic/period, finite source, local $p$-adic, and
determinant assembly) and ask, column by column, which data a given
number remembers.  The test is a finite Schur complement, which vanishes
exactly when the desired readout is a linear function of the admitted
data.  The full data of each of the first four columns are adequate for
their readouts, and the five columns together are adequate for an
orientation-normalized Bloch--Kato scalar, which forgets the orientation.
Along the way we fix three normalization points: the invariant finite
factor is the order of a paired group, not a numerical Pfaffian of its
Cassels--Tate pairing; on the finite local condition the relevant map is
the logarithm, not the dual exponential; and the assembled value must not
be reused as a factor.  We then identify readouts that lose information.
The most concrete is arithmetic: the curves $571a1$ and $2045b1$ have the
same rank, Tamagawa numbers, torsion and $\dim\Sha[2]$, but $2$-primary
Tate--Shafarevich groups of orders $4$ and $16$.  Other results, proved on
explicit models, concern the scalar finite factor, Gram matrices in rank
at least two, and finite lists of curves and of primes.  We also identify
the normalization $\kapr=\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ under which a
cascade form of the leading term agrees with the standard one.  The
comparisons from the columns to the Bloch--Kato line are stated as
external inputs.  Nothing here proves BSD.  Many statements are checked
in Lean~4, and each is labelled by the exact extent of that check.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}


For an elliptic curve $E/\Q$ of analytic rank $r_{\mathrm{an}}$, the
Strong Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer
formula~\citep{BirchSwinnertonDyer1965,Tate1966bsd} compares two numbers:
\[
  \frac{L^{(r_{\mathrm{an}})}(E,1)}{r_{\mathrm{an}}!}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \Omega_E\,\Reg(E/\Q)\,
  \frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Tam(E)}{\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}} .
\]
The right-hand side is visibly a product of pieces of different kinds: a
period, a regulator computed from heights of rational points, Tamagawa
numbers, torsion, and the order of the Tate--Shafarevich group.  In the
Bloch--Kato formulation~\citep{BlochKato1990,FontainePerrinRiou1994} these
pieces are assembled into a single determinant line, the fundamental
line, together with local $p$-adic data and sign and orientation
conventions.

This paper does not ask whether the formula is true.  It asks a more
modest question about its ingredients: each factor is the image of richer
data (a lattice with a pairing, a finite group with a pairing, a local
Galois representation), and one may ask which of those data a given
number still remembers.  We separate the ingredients into five
\emph{columns}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item height/regulator: the Mordell--Weil lattice and its N\'eron--Tate
  pairing;
\item analytic/period: the leading coefficient of $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$ and the
  real period;
\item finite source: Tamagawa numbers, torsion, the $p$-primary parts of
  $\Sha$ and the Cassels--Tate pairing;
\item local $p$-adic: data at a prime $p$, of two kinds treated here,
  ordinary data at a prime of good ordinary reduction and signed data at an
  odd prime of good supersingular reduction with $a_p=0$;
\item determinant assembly: the orientation and sign conventions used to
  multiply the other four in one line.
\end{enumerate}
For each column we test whether its native data are enough to recover the
intended readout, and we identify readouts that are not.

\paragraph{The adequacy residual.}
All tests in the paper use one finite-dimensional device.  Let $x\in\R^n$
be coordinates for the data of a column, let $D:\R^n\to\R^m$ be the
\emph{readout} (the quantity one wants), and let $L:\R^n\to\R^k$ be the
\emph{source} (the data one admits).  With the standard inner product on
$\R^n$ (the matrix $C=I$ that appears in the statements below), form the
Gram blocks
\[
  \KLL=LL^{\mathsf T},\qquad \KDL=DL^{\mathsf T},\qquad \KDD=DD^{\mathsf T},
\]
and the Schur complement~\citep{Zhang2005schur,HornJohnson2013}
\[
  \XiC=\KDD-\KDL\,\Kdagger\,K_{LD},
\]
where $\Kdagger$ is the Moore--Penrose inverse.  If $P_L$ is the orthogonal
projector onto the row space of $L$, then $\XiC=D(I-P_L)D^{\mathsf T}$,
a positive semidefinite matrix.  It vanishes exactly when $D(I-P_L)=0$,
that is, when every row of $D$ lies in the row space of $L$, or
equivalently when $D=ML$ for some matrix $M$.  We say that the source is
\emph{adequate} for the readout when $\XiC=0$: the readout is then a
linear function of the admitted data.  When $D$ is the identity, $\XiC=I-P_L$
and its trace counts the directions the source does not see.

In this language the results are small, explicit computations.  For
example, the readout of a scalar finite factor is a single row
$s=(1,-2,1,0)$ in four finite coordinates, and $\tr(I_4-P_s)=4-1=3$; at
a supersingular prime, a source that keeps the signed comparison
coordinate but replaces the two signed coordinates by their sum leaves
$\tr(I_3-P_{\mathrm{uns}})=3-2=1$.  The calculations are elementary
by design.  Their purpose is bookkeeping: they record which coordinates
must be present before a datum can be used as a source for a given
readout.

\paragraph{An example.}
For the curve $11a1$~\citep{Cremona1997,LMFDB},
\[
  \frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_E}=0.2
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \frac{c_{11}\,\#\Sha(E/\Q)}{\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}}
  =\frac{5\cdot1}{5^2}=0.2 .
\]
As a scalar check this is one equality.  In the five columns it is several
facts: the height column is empty (rank $0$); the finite column records
that the value $5/25$ comes from a Tamagawa number and from torsion, not
from $\Sha$; and the local columns record, prime by prime, which local
theory applies.  The scalar equality is compatible with, but coarser
than, these facts.  A valuation can sit in the Tamagawa factor or in
$\Sha$ and contribute the same exponent to the scalar; two signed local
directions can have the same unsigned sum; a Gram matrix computes the
regulator but says nothing about where the points come from.

\paragraph{Results.}
\Cref{sec:decomposition} sets up the five columns, the assembly into the
Bloch--Kato line, and an additive splitting of the comparison defect.
\Cref{sec:height-regulator,sec:analytic-period,sec:finite-source,sec:p-adic,sec:det-assembly}
treat the columns one at a time.  In the first four, the full native data
are adequate for the column readout; in the assembly column, the four
arithmetic columns fall short of the assembled target by exactly the
orientation direction.  Each column section ends with the comparison to
the Bloch--Kato line that the column requires as external input.  Along the way
we fix three normalization points: the order of a paired finite group,
not a numerical Pfaffian of its pairing, is the invariant finite factor;
on the finite local condition the relevant map is the logarithm, not the
dual exponential; and the orientation enters the assembly as an
independent input, so that the assembled value must not be reused as a
factor.  \Cref{sec:comparison} contracts the five columns to an
orientation-normalized scalar readout and shows that the typed data are
adequate for it, while this readout forgets the orientation coordinate;
identifying it with the arithmetic Bloch--Kato scalar requires the five
transports and, in addition, a convention making that scalar independent
of the orientation.

\Cref{sec:finite-source,sec:higher-rank-no-go,sec:global-audit-no-go,sec:support-prime-no-go}
contain the ``does not determine'' results (\Cref{tab:no-go-suite}).  The
most concrete is arithmetic: the curves $571a1$ and $2045b1$ have the same
rank, Tamagawa numbers, torsion and $\dim\Sha[2]=2$, but $2$-primary
Tate--Shafarevich groups of orders $4$ and $16$
(\Cref{ex:apparatus:sha-pair}).  The others are formal and are proved on
explicit models: the scalar finite factor forgets which block carries a
valuation; the Gram matrix forgets the provenance of a basis; and finite
lists of curves or of primes do not decide statements about all curves or
all primes.  \Cref{sec:vsrc} introduces the exterior algebra $\vsrc_r$ as
the formal shape of a rank-$r$ source, and \Cref{sec:kappa-normalization}
identifies the constant $\kapr=\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ that makes a
cascade form of the leading term agree with the standard one.
\Cref{sec:scope} lists what the paper does not claim.

\paragraph{Context.}
The vocabulary of columns, readouts and residuals is a specialization of
the adequacy calculus of the Six Birds
framework~\citep{TsiokosNeedles2026,TsiokosFoundationsII2026,TsiokosFoundationsIII2026}.
No knowledge of that framework is assumed; the definitions above are all
that is used.  The companion paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDClosure2026} uses the
present apparatus, in particular the normalization of $\kapr$, in a
conditional treatment of the scalar BSD identity.  Many statements are
checked in Lean~4 without external mathematical libraries; the footnotes
say, statement by statement, whether Lean proves the statement as written,
a narrower model version, or only records it as a conditional schema, and
\Cref{app:formalization} gives the details.
 \section{Decomposition: the typed five columns}
\label{sec:decomposition}

The introduction separated the BSD product into five kinds of data.  We now
make the separation precise.  The aim is not to replace the Bloch--Kato
determinant line by five unrelated objects.  It is to keep track of five
native sources \emph{before} they are multiplied together, so that each can
be tested on its own.

Fix an elliptic curve $E/\Q$ and put $M_E=h^1(E)(1)$.  The five columns are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the \emph{height/regulator} column: the Mordell--Weil lattice and its
  N\'eron--Tate pairing;
\item the \emph{analytic/period} column: the leading Taylor coefficient of
  $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$ and the real period;
\item the \emph{finite-source} column: Tamagawa numbers, rational torsion,
  the $p$-primary parts of $\Sha$, and the Cassels--Tate pairing;
\item for each prime $p$, the \emph{local $p$-adic} column: ordinary or
  signed local data at $p$, in the cases treated in \Cref{sec:p-adic};
\item the \emph{determinant-assembly} column: the orientation, sign and unit
  conventions needed to place the other four columns in one determinant
  line.
\end{enumerate}

and the common adequacy test applied to each of them.

The Bloch--Kato target is the determinant-line package attached to
$M_E$~\citep{Deligne1987,BlochKato1990,FontainePerrinRiou1994}: the motive,
its fundamental line, the zeta element, and a chosen trivialization.  The
source object is the \emph{integrated BSD carrier}, which keeps the five
kinds of data in separate slots.  The first structural map assembles the
source into the target.

\begin{definition}[Bloch--Kato component-assembly map]
\label{def:apparatus:bk-component-map}
For $M_E=h^1(E)(1)$, let
\[
  C_{\mathrm{BSD}}(E)
  =
  \bigl(C_{\mathrm{ht}}(E),C_{\mathrm{an}}(E),C_{\mathrm{fin}}(E),
  \{C_p(E)\}_p,C_{\det}(E)\bigr)
\]
be the integrated BSD carrier, with height/regulator, analytic/period,
finite-source, local $p$-adic and determinant-assembly slots.  The
source-side slot $C_{\det}(E)$ holds an orientation datum and is distinct
from the Bloch--Kato target
\[
  C_{\mathrm{BK}}(E)
  =
  \bigl(M_E,\Delta_f(M_E),z_{\mathrm{BK}}(M_E),\tau_{\mathrm{BK}}(M_E)\bigr).
\]
Suppose given column maps $\rho_{\mathrm{ht}}$, $\rho_{\mathrm{an}}$,
$\rho_{\mathrm{fin}}$, $\rho_p$ and $\rho_{\det}$ into the target, and an
assembly operation that combines one value from each column.  The
component-assembly map
\[
  \rho_{\mathrm{BK}}
  =
  \operatorname{assemble}\Bigl(
  \rho_{\mathrm{ht}},\rho_{\mathrm{an}},\rho_{\mathrm{fin}},
  (\rho_p)_p,\rho_{\det}\Bigr)
  :
  C_{\mathrm{BSD}}(E)\longrightarrow C_{\mathrm{BK}}(E)
\]
applies each column map to its own slot and then assembles the results.
Informally, $\rho_{\mathrm{ht}}$ sends height and regulator data to the
global cohomological side; $\rho_{\mathrm{an}}$ sends the leading term and
period to the analytic (zeta-element) side; $\rho_{\mathrm{fin}}$ sends
Tamagawa, torsion, $\Sha[p^\infty]$ and Cassels--Tate data to the finite
determinant side; $\rho_p$ sends local $p$-adic data to the $p$-primary
side; and $\rho_{\det}$ supplies the orientation in the common line.
\end{definition}

The definition organizes the source; it constructs neither the arithmetic
column maps nor the assembly operation.  Those are exactly the five
comparison transports recorded as obligations in the column sections below
(\Cref{obl:apparatus:rho-ht-reg-transport,obl:apparatus:rho-an-period-transport,obl:apparatus:rho-finite-transport,obl:apparatus:rho-p-transport,obl:apparatus:rho-det-assembly}).
Once a common trivialization is chosen, the total comparison defect can be
split into one contribution per column.

\begin{definition}[Bridge-defect equation]
\label{def:apparatus:bridge-defect-equation}
Choose a trivialization of the determinant-line target in which the
component comparisons take values in a common additive group, and write
$\Delta_{\mathrm{BSD}}^{\mathrm{BK}}$ for the total defect of the BSD
comparison.  The bridge-defect equation is the decomposition
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{BSD}}^{\mathrm{BK}}
  =
  E_{\mathrm{ht/reg}}
  +E_{\mathrm{an/period}}
  +E_{\mathrm{finite}}
  +\sum_p E_p
  +E_{\det}
\]
into the height/regulator, analytic/period, finite-source, local and
determinant-assembly defects.  The local sum is required to be finite:
only finitely many $E_p$ are nonzero, and the sum of the local defects is
zero when every $E_p$ is zero.  Passing between this additive form and
the multiplicative form (a total ratio equal to the product of five
component ratios) requires a logarithm or trivialization convention that
also accounts for signs and units; we do not fix one here.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Component-killing]
\label{thm:apparatus:component-killing}
Assume the bridge-defect equation holds in the chosen trivialization.  If
\[
  E_{\mathrm{ht/reg}}=0,\qquad
  E_{\mathrm{an/period}}=0,\qquad
  E_{\mathrm{finite}}=0,\qquad
  E_p=0\ \text{for every }p,\qquad
  E_{\det}=0,
\]
then $\Delta_{\mathrm{BSD}}^{\mathrm{BK}}=0$.  Equivalently, if
$\Delta_{\mathrm{BSD}}^{\mathrm{BK}}\neq 0$, then either some component
defect is nonzero or the componentwise comparison (the bridge-defect
equation together with its finiteness condition) is not available.\footnote{Lean proves the integer-valued implication, with a supplied
value for the local sum and the explicit hypothesis that this value is
zero when every $E_p$ is zero; finiteness of the support is one way to
guarantee that hypothesis.  Declaration names for all formal statements
are collected in \Cref{app:formalization}.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the finiteness condition, $\sum_pE_p=0$ when every $E_p$ vanishes.
Substituting the five vanishing defects into the bridge-defect equation
gives $\Delta_{\mathrm{BSD}}^{\mathrm{BK}}=0$.  The second statement is
the contrapositive.
\end{proof}

The next result is the first of several statements in this paper of the
form ``a public number does not determine the typed datum behind it''.  It
concerns the scalar finite factor of the BSD formula.

\begin{theorem}[The finite scalar factor forgets where a valuation sits]
\label{thm:apparatus:finite-scalar-public-shadow}
Fix a prime $p$.  A \emph{finite-source record} at $p$ is a triple of
integers $(s,t,u)$, standing for
$\bigl(v_p(\#\Sha),\,v_p(\prod_\ell c_\ell),\,v_p(\#E(\Q)_{\tors})\bigr)$,
with $s$ even, together with a Cassels--Tate datum $\theta$ from some set
with at least two elements.  The valuation of the scalar finite factor
\[
  \phi_{\mathrm{fin}}
  =
  \frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\prod_\ell c_\ell}{\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}}
\]
defines the readout $q_p(s,t,u,\theta)=s+t-2u$ on records.  This readout
is not injective: distinct records with different nonzero blocks have the
same value, and $q_p$ does not depend on $\theta$ at all.\footnote{Lean proves the statement for this model of the tower, with a
two-valued Cassels--Tate tag.  Whether the two records below are realized
by elliptic curves is not addressed; \Cref{sec:finite-source} gives an
arithmetic example for a different readout.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The parity condition on $s$ reflects the fact that, when $\Sha(E/\Q)$ is
finite, its order is a square, because the Cassels--Tate pairing is
alternating and nondegenerate~\citep{Cassels1962,PoonenStoll1999}.  The
records $(2,0,0,\theta)$ and $(0,2,0,\theta)$ are distinct: the first
places the valuation in the $\Sha$ block, the second in the Tamagawa
block.  Both give $q_p=2$.  Finally $q_p$ depends only on $(s,t,u)$, so
changing $\theta$ does not change it.
\end{proof}

For a curve with finite $\Sha$, the formula for $q_p$ is the $p$-adic
valuation of the arithmetic factor $\phi_{\mathrm{fin}}$.  The
noninjectivity proved above concerns formal records; we do not assert here
that its two witnesses are realized by curves.

The theorem concerns a specific readout.  It does not say that no richer
arithmetic theorem, given additional source data, can recover the finite
datum.  This qualification applies to every ``does not determine'' result
in the paper, and we will not repeat it each time.

\begin{nonclaim}[Decomposition scope]
\label{nonclaim:apparatus:decomposition-scope}
This section does not prove BSD, the Bloch--Kato conjecture, or the
equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture~\citep{BurnsFlach2001}.  It defines
the five-column assembly, proves the component-killing consequence of the
bridge-defect equation, and shows that the scalar finite factor forgets
information.  The comparison transports from the five native columns to
the determinant-line target are external arithmetic inputs, recorded
separately as obligations; they are not derived here.
\end{nonclaim}
 \section{Height/regulator column}
\label{sec:height-regulator}

The height/regulator column keeps the Mordell--Weil lattice and its
N\'eron--Tate pairing~\citep{Neron1965,Silverman2009} before any comparison
with the global determinant line.  Its readout is the regulator, viewed as
the coefficient of a determinant.

\begin{definition}[Real height-regulator map]\label{def:apparatus:height-regulator-map}
Let
\[
  \Lambda_E=E(\Q)/E(\Q)_{\tors},
  \qquad
  V_E=\Lambda_E\otimes_{\Z}\R,
\]
and let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}$ be the N\'eron--Tate
pairing on $V_E$, with associated map
\[
  h_{\mathrm{NT}}:V_E\longrightarrow V_E^*,
  \qquad
  P\longmapsto \langle P,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}.
\]
The real height-regulator map sends the triple
$(V_E,\Lambda_E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}})$ to
\[
  \rho_{\mathrm{ht/reg}}^{\R}
  =\det(h_{\mathrm{NT}})\in\det(V_E^*)\otimes\det(V_E)^*.
\]
If $(P_1,\ldots,P_r)$ is a $\Z$-basis of $\Lambda_E$ and
$H_E=\bigl(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}\bigr)_{1\le i,j\le r}$ is
the Gram matrix, the coefficient of this element in the induced basis is
$\det H_E=\Reg(E/\Q)$, with the empty determinant equal to $1$ when $r=0$.
The coefficient does not depend on the basis: a change of basis by
$A\in\mathrm{GL}_r(\Z)$ replaces $H_E$ by $A^{\mathsf T}H_EA$, and
$\det(A^{\mathsf T}H_EA)=\det(A)^2\det H_E=\det H_E$ because
$\det A=\pm1$.  (A change of basis of finite index $m>1$ multiplies the
coefficient by $m^2$, so integrality of the basis matters.)
\end{definition}

The adequacy test for this column asks whether the full height matrix is
enough to recover the first-order variation of $\log\Reg$.  It is, for a
simple reason: the readout is a linear function of the variation, and the
full source sees every coordinate of the variation.

\begin{theorem}[Height tangent Schur collapse]\label{thm:apparatus:height-schur-collapse}
Let $r\ge1$ and let $H$ be a positive definite real symmetric $r\times r$
matrix.  Identify a symmetric variation $\delta H$ with its coordinate
vector $x=\operatorname{vech}(\delta H)\in\R^n$, $n=r(r+1)/2$, listing the
entries $\delta h_{ij}$ with $i\le j$.  Then
\[
  D_H(\delta H)
  =
  d(\log\det)_H(\delta H)
  =
  \tr(H^{-1}\delta H)
  =
  d_Hx,
\]
where the row $d_H$ has entry $(H^{-1})_{ii}$ at each diagonal
coordinate and $2(H^{-1})_{ij}$ at each off-diagonal coordinate $i<j$.
Take the audit energy $C=I_n$ on these coordinates and the full source
$L_{\mathrm{full}}(x)=x$.  Then
\[
  \KDD=\|d_H\|^2,
  \qquad
  \XiC=d_Hd_H^{\mathsf T}-d_H\,I_n\,d_H^{\mathsf T}=0 .
\]
For $r=0$ the tangent space is zero, $n=0$, and $\KDD=0$: the rank-zero
row is vacuous.\footnote{Lean proves the algebraic core: for every row $d$ of every
length over any commutative ring, the identity source gives Schur
residual zero, and the identity matrix is the unique matrix satisfying
the four Penrose equations at the identity.  For $r=2$ it also proves
that the displayed row computes $\tr(H^{-1}\delta H)$ when
$\det H\neq0$.  The calculus step identifying the row with the derivative
of $\log\det$, and the general-rank row, are proved here on paper.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $H$ is invertible, $\det(H+t\,\delta H)=\det H\cdot\det(I+tA)$ with
$A=H^{-1}\delta H$.  In the permutation expansion of $\det(I+tA)$, the
identity permutation contributes $1+t\tr A+O(t^2)$, and every other
permutation moves at least two indices and contributes $O(t^2)$.  Since
$\det H>0$, the chain rule gives $d(\log\det)_H(\delta H)=\tr(H^{-1}\delta
H)$.  Writing $G=H^{-1}$, which is symmetric,
\[
  \tr(G\,\delta H)=\sum_{i,j}G_{ij}\,\delta h_{ji}
  =\sum_i G_{ii}\,\delta h_{ii}+\sum_{i<j}2G_{ij}\,\delta h_{ij},
\]
because the off-diagonal coordinate $\delta h_{ij}$ occurs twice in the
symmetric matrix $\delta H$.  This is $d_Hx$.

For the Schur residual, the source block is $\KLL=I_n$, whose
Moore--Penrose inverse is $I_n$, and the cross blocks are $\KDL=d_H$ and
$K_{LD}=d_H^{\mathsf T}$.  Hence
$\KDL\Kdagger K_{LD}=d_H\,I_n\,d_H^{\mathsf T}=d_Hd_H^{\mathsf T}=\KDD$,
and $\XiC=0$.  If $r=0$, the row $d_H$ is empty and the empty sum
$d_Hd_H^{\mathsf T}$ is $0$.
\end{proof}

For example, when $r=2$ and $H=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
b&c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with $\Delta=ac-b^2>0$, the row in the
coordinates $(\delta h_{11},\delta h_{12},\delta h_{22})$ is
\[
  d_H=\Bigl(\frac{c}{\Delta},\,-\frac{2b}{\Delta},\,\frac{a}{\Delta}\Bigr),
\]
as one also sees from $\det(H+t\,\delta H)=\Delta+t\,(c\,\delta h_{11}-2b\,
\delta h_{12}+a\,\delta h_{22})+O(t^2)$.  When $r=1$, $d_H=1/\Reg(E/\Q)$
and $\KDD=\Reg(E/\Q)^{-2}$; for the curve $37a1$, the regulator
$0.0511114\ldots$ recorded in the LMFDB~\citep{LMFDB} gives
$\KDD=382.79\ldots$.  The choice $C=I_n$ refers to the chosen
$\operatorname{vech}$ coordinates; it is not invariant under changes of
lattice basis, and only the vanishing of $\XiC$, not the value of $\KDD$,
is used later.

\begin{obligation}[Height/regulator transport]\label{obl:apparatus:rho-ht-reg-transport}
The Bloch--Kato height/regulator component requires a comparison
\[
  \beta_{\mathrm{BK,ht}}:
  \det(V_E^*)\otimes\det(V_E)^*
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta_f(M_E)_{\mathrm{ht/reg}}
\]
identifying the N\'eron--Tate determinant with the Beilinson--Deligne
regulator normalization~\citep{Beilinson1985}.  It must fix
the coefficient and lattice conventions, the archimedean
Deligne-cohomology normalization, and the orientation and
trivialization, and it must be compatible with the finite and
determinant-assembly columns without absorbing them.  This comparison is
external arithmetic input.  It is not derived from the finite calculation
above, which only shows that the full height matrix is adequate for the
first-order regulator readout.
\end{obligation}

\begin{warning}[Rank-zero carriers]\label{warn:apparatus:571a1-carrier}
For a curve of rank $0$ the height/regulator column is empty: there is no
nonzero height variation, and the regulator is the empty determinant $1$.
The curve $571a1$, which reappears in \Cref{sec:finite-source} because
its Tate--Shafarevich group has nontrivial $2$-primary part, is such a
curve: a certified $2$-descent bounds its rank above and below by $0$
(details in \Cref{sec:finite-source}).  It should not be confused with
the rank-two curve $571b1$ of the same conductor.  Rank-two
height/regulator examples in this paper use $389a1$.
\end{warning}
 \section{Analytic/period column}
\label{sec:analytic-period}

The analytic/period column keeps the analytic leading coefficient and the
real period apart from the height, finite and local data.  Its native data
form a pair of numbers, and its readout is their quotient.

\begin{definition}[Real analytic/period map]\label{def:apparatus:analytic-period-map}
By modularity~\citep{Wiles1995,BreuilConradDiamondTaylor2001},
$L(E,s)=L(f_E,s)$ extends to an entire function.  Let
$r_{\mathrm{an}}=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$ and
\[
  A_E=\frac{L^{(r_{\mathrm{an}})}(E,1)}{r_{\mathrm{an}}!}\neq0
\]
be the analytic leading coefficient, that is, the leading Taylor
coefficient of $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$.  Let $\Omega_E^+>0$ be the real period
in the BSD normalization: the integral of $|\omega_E|$ over $E(\R)$ for a
N\'eron differential $\omega_E$.  The native analytic/period family is the
pair
\[
  L_{\mathrm{an/per}}(E)=(A_E,\Omega_E^+),
\]
and the real analytic/period map is
\[
  \rho_{\mathrm{an/period}}^{\R}:(A_E,\Omega_E^+)\longmapsto
  (A_E/\Omega_E^+)\,e_{\mathrm{an/per}},
\]
where $e_{\mathrm{an/per}}$ is a basis vector of a real line.  The column
contains no height, torsion, Tamagawa or $\Sha$ data.
\end{definition}

The adequacy test asks whether the pair is enough to recover the first-order
variation of the quotient.  In logarithmic coordinates the absolute value
of the quotient becomes a difference; its sign is not a tangent direction
and is recorded separately, as part of the orientation data of
\Cref{sec:det-assembly}.

\begin{theorem}[Analytic/period tangent Schur collapse]\label{thm:apparatus:analytic-period-schur-collapse}
In the chart
\[
  x=(x_A,x_\Omega)=(\log|A_E|,\log\Omega_E^+)\in\R^2,
\]
take $C=I_2$, the full native source $L_{\mathrm{full}}(x)=x$, and the
readout $D(x)=x_A-x_\Omega=\log|A_E/\Omega_E^+|=dx$ with $d=(1,-1)$.  Then $\KLL=I_2$,
$\KDD=dd^{\mathsf T}=2$ and
\[
  \XiC=2-d\,I_2\,d^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]
With no source admitted the residual is $2$; with only $x_A$ admitted it
is $1$; with both coordinates admitted it is $0$.\footnote{Lean proves these three integer residual computations for the
row $(1,-1)$.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The readout energy is $\KDD=1^2+(-1)^2=2$.  With the full source,
$\KLL=\Kdagger=I_2$, $\KDL=d$ and $K_{LD}=d^{\mathsf T}$, so the projected
energy is $d\,I_2\,d^{\mathsf T}=2$ and $\XiC=0$.  With no source nothing is
projected away and the residual is $2$.  If only $x_A$ is admitted, $d$
splits orthogonally as the seen part $(1,0)$ plus the unseen part
$(0,-1)$, and the residual is the squared length $1$ of the unseen part.
\end{proof}

The theorem is elementary, and that is the point: a single observed
coordinate cannot recover a quotient of two independent quantities,
while the pair can.  The arithmetic content of the column lies entirely in
the comparison below.

\begin{obligation}[Analytic/period transport]\label{obl:apparatus:rho-an-period-transport}
The Bloch--Kato analytic component requires a comparison
\[
  \beta_{\mathrm{BK,an}}:
  L_{\mathrm{an/per}}(E)_{\R}
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta_f(M_E)_{\mathrm{an/per}}
\]
identifying $A_E/\Omega_E^+$ with the zeta-element factor of the
fundamental line.  It must fix the rational and integral normalizations,
the Betti--de Rham comparison and the real orientation, the leading-term
convention in positive rank, and the separation from the other four
columns.  We treat this comparison as external input in every rank.  In
rank $0$ the analytic quotient $L(E,1)/\Omega_E^+$ is a classical object,
but matching it to the zeta-element factor in the conventions above is
part of the comparison, not a consequence of the Schur calculation.
\end{obligation}
 \section{Finite-source column}
\label{sec:finite-source}

The finite-source column keeps the finite arithmetic data before they are
contracted to the scalar finite factor
$\#\Sha\prod_vc_v/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$.  Its tower separates Tamagawa
numbers~\citep{Silverman1994}, rational torsion, the $p$-primary parts of
$\Sha$, and the Cassels--Tate pairing~\citep{Cassels1962,PoonenStoll1999}.
The pairing is part of the source: it determines the structure of each
$p$-primary group, while the scalar factor only sees orders.

\begin{definition}[Real finite-source map]\label{def:apparatus:finite-source-map}
The finite-source tower is
$C_{\mathrm{fin}}(E)=C_{\mathrm{Tam}}\oplus C_{\tors}\oplus
C_{\Sha[p^\infty]}\oplus C_{\mathrm{CT}}$, with native family
\[
  L_{\mathrm{finite}}(E)
  =
  \Bigl((c_v(E))_{v\mid N},\ E(\Q)_{\tors},\
  \bigl(S_p,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{CT},p}\bigr)_p\Bigr),
  \qquad
  S_p=\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]/\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]_{\mathrm{div}},
\]
where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{CT},p}:S_p\times S_p\to\Q/\Z$ is the
perfect alternating Cassels--Tate pairing.  Throughout this section we
assume that $\Sha(E/\Q)$ is finite.  Then each divisible part is zero,
$S_p=\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]$, only finitely many $S_p$ are nontrivial, and
$\prod_p\#S_p=\#\Sha(E/\Q)$.  The real finite-source map is the product
of three factors:
\[
  \rho_{\mathrm{finite}}^{\R}
  =
  \rho_{\mathrm{Tam}}^{\R}
  \otimes
  \rho_{\tors}^{\R}
  \otimes
  \bigotimes_p \rho_{\Sha\text{-}\mathrm{CT},p}^{\R},
\]
with $\rho_{\mathrm{Tam}}^{\R}=\prod_{v\mid N}c_v$, where
$c_v=\#E(\Q_v)/E^0(\Q_v)$; $\rho_{\tors}^{\R}=\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{-2}$; and
\[
  \rho_{\Sha\text{-}\mathrm{CT},p}^{\R}\bigl(S_p,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{CT},p}\bigr)=\#S_p .
\]
The last factor has an integral description.  If an alternating integer
matrix $A$ presents $S_p$ (that is, $S_p\cong\operatorname{coker}A$) with
linking form $(x,y)\mapsto -x^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}y \bmod \Z$ equal to the
pairing, then $\#S_p=|\det A|=\Pf(A)^2$.  For example, the hyperbolic
plane $H_n=(\Z/n\Z)^2$ with
$\langle(a,b),(c,d)\rangle=(ad-bc)/n \bmod \Z$ is presented by
$A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&n\\-n&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, with
$|\Pf(A)|=n$ and $\#H_n=n^2$.
\end{definition}

Three numbers attached to $H_n$ should be kept apart: the coefficient $1/n$
of the pairing on a chosen basis, the integral Pfaffian $n$ (up to sign),
and the order $n^2$.  Only the last enters the BSD formula.  The first is
not an invariant of the paired group: the rational lifts $1/2$ and $3/2$
of the same class in $\Q/\Z$ have different ``Pfaffians'', and on $H_4$
the automorphism $(a,b)\mapsto(3a,b)$ changes the coefficient on the new
basis from $1/4$ to $3/4$.  The integral presentation removes this
ambiguity: a unimodular change of basis $B$ replaces $A$ by
$B^{\mathsf T}AB$, which changes $\Pf(A)$ by the sign $\det B$ and leaves
$\Pf(A)^2$ unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Pairing-aware finite Schur collapse]\label{thm:apparatus:finite-source-schur-collapse}
At a prime $p$, use the finite tangent coordinates
$x=(x_T,x_{\tors},x_{\Sha},x_{\mathrm{CT}})\in\R^4$, where
$x_T=v_p(\prod_vc_v)$, $x_{\tors}=v_p(\#E(\Q)_{\tors})$,
$x_{\Sha}=v_p(\#S_p)$, and $x_{\mathrm{CT}}$ is a coordinate for the
Cassels--Tate datum.  With the full readout $D_{\mathrm{full}}(x)=x$, the
full source $L_{\mathrm{full}}(x)=x$ and $C=I_4$,
\[
  \XiC=I_4-I_4I_4^{\dagger}I_4=0.
\]
Thus the full typed finite tower is adequate for the full finite
readout.\footnote{Lean proves the identity calculation on the four declared
coordinates.  It does not model the finite groups themselves; those
enter in \Cref{thm:apparatus:dim-sha-shadow}.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All four blocks $\KDD,\KLL,\KDL,K_{LD}$ equal $I_4$, and the
Moore--Penrose inverse of $I_4$ is $I_4$.  Hence
$\KDL\Kdagger K_{LD}=I_4$ and $\XiC=I_4-I_4=0$.
\end{proof}

The two results below concern readouts that keep less.  The first is the
scalar finite factor; the second keeps only the size of the $p$-torsion
of $\Sha$.

\begin{theorem}[The scalar finite row leaves a three-dimensional residual]\label{thm:apparatus:scalar-finite-shadow}
In the coordinates of \Cref{thm:apparatus:finite-source-schur-collapse},
the valuation of the scalar finite factor is $q=s\cdot x$ with
$s=(1,-2,1,0)$.  The orthogonal projector onto this row is
$P_s=s^{\mathsf T}(ss^{\mathsf T})^{-1}s$, and the residual against the
full readout is
\[
  \Xi_{\mathrm{scalar}}=I_4-P_s,
  \qquad
  \tr(\Xi_{\mathrm{scalar}})=3.
\]
The readout is not injective: the coordinate vectors $(0,0,2,h)$, for
any $h$, and $(2,0,0,0)$ are distinct and both give $q=2$.\footnote{Lean proves the projector trace, the residual trace and the
two evaluations over $\Q$.  The vectors are formal coordinates; as in
\Cref{thm:apparatus:finite-scalar-public-shadow}, arithmetic realization
is not asserted.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Here $ss^{\mathsf T}=1+4+1+0=6$, so $(P_s)_{ij}=s_is_j/6$ and
$\tr P_s=6/6=1$.  Hence $\tr(I_4-P_s)=4-1=3$.  The two evaluations are
$q(0,0,2,h)=2$ and $q(2,0,0,0)=2$; the coordinate $h$ does not enter
because the last entry of $s$ is $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-torsion size does not determine the paired group]\label{thm:apparatus:dim-sha-shadow}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item On the two-coordinate model $y=(m,\theta)$, where $m$ records the
  $\mathbb F_p$-dimension of the $p$-torsion and $\theta$ the remaining
  structure of the paired group, the dimension-only source
  $L_{\dim}(y)=(1,0)y$ leaves the residual
  $\Xi_{\dim}=I_2-(1,0)^{\mathsf T}(1,0)=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$, of
  trace $1$.
\item The groups $H_2=(\Z/2\Z)^2$ and $H_4=(\Z/4\Z)^2$, with the pairings
  $\langle(a,b),(c,d)\rangle=(ad-bc)/n\bmod\Z$, are finite abelian groups
  with perfect alternating pairings.  Their $2$-torsion subgroups both
  have $\mathbb F_2$-dimension $2$, but their orders are $4$ and $16$.
  Hence no function of the $2$-torsion dimension recovers the order.
\item Conversely, $H_4$ and $H_2\oplus H_2$ both have order $16$ and
  carry perfect alternating pairings, but their $2$-torsion subgroups
  have dimensions $2$ and $4$.  Hence no function of the order recovers
  the $2$-torsion dimension.\footnote{Lean proves the coordinate-model calculation in (1) and the
finite-group statement (2), with perfectness checked against every
additive character into the cyclic target; (3) is proved in a separate
Lean module.  The arithmetic example below uses inputs from outside
Lean.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (1), the dimension source keeps the first coordinate and forgets the
second, so its projector is $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and the residual is
$\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$.  For (2), $H_n$ is presented by
$A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&n\\-n&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, and the
pairing is perfect because its matrix on the standard basis,
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1/n\\-1/n&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, induces
an isomorphism $H_n\to\operatorname{Hom}(H_n,\frac1n\Z/\Z)$.  The
$2$-torsion of $H_2$ is $H_2$ itself, and the $2$-torsion of $H_4$ is
$(2\Z/4\Z)^2\cong(\Z/2\Z)^2$; both have dimension $2$.  The orders are
$4$ and $16$.  For (3), the orthogonal sum of two perfect alternating
pairings is perfect and alternating, $\#(H_2\oplus H_2)=16$, and its
$2$-torsion is the whole group, of dimension $4$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is about finite paired groups.  The next example shows that
the loss it describes occurs for actual Tate--Shafarevich groups, even
when several other finite invariants are held fixed.

\begin{example}[Matched coarse data, different $2$-primary $\Sha$]\label{ex:apparatus:sha-pair}
Consider the curves
\[
\begin{aligned}
  571a1:&\quad y^2+y=x^3-x^2-929x-10595,\\
  2045b1:&\quad y^2+xy=x^3-x^2-5470x-862675.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their invariants compare as follows.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
 & $571a1$ & $2045b1$ \\
\midrule
rank of $E(\Q)$ & $0$ & $0$ \\
Tamagawa numbers & all $1$ & all $1$ \\
$E(\Q)_{\tors}$ & trivial & trivial \\
$\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\Sha(E/\Q)[2]$ & $2$ & $2$ \\
$\Sha(E/\Q)[2^\infty]$ & $(\Z/2\Z)^2$ & $(\Z/4\Z)^2$ \\
$\#\Sha(E/\Q)$ & $4u$, $u$ odd & $16v$, $v$ odd \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
So every function of the rank, the Tamagawa numbers, the torsion group
and $\dim\Sha[2]$ takes the same value on the two curves, while their
$2$-primary $\Sha$ groups, their orders of $\Sha$, and hence their scalar
finite factors $\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ differ.  The two
curves are not matched on everything: their analytic quotients
$|L(E,1)|/\Omega_E^+$ are $4$ and $16$.
\end{example}

The entries of the table are established as follows; we indicate which
inputs are external to this paper.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Rank $0$ and finiteness of $\Sha$.}  Exact modular-symbol
  computations with PARI/GP~\citep{PARI2023} give $L(E,1)\neq0$ for both
  curves.  By modularity~\citep{Wiles1995,BreuilConradDiamondTaylor2001},
  Gross--Zagier~\citep{GrossZagier1986} and
  Kolyvagin~\citep{Kolyvagin1990}, both curves have rank $0$ and finite
  $\Sha$.  The absolute values $|L(E,1)|/\Omega_E^+=4$ and $16$ follow
  from the primitive integral modular symbol once its scale is fixed;
  the curves are semistable and optimal, so their Manin constant is $1$
  by \citet[Theorem~1.2]{Cesnavicius2018}.
\item \emph{Local data.}  The curve $571a1$ has reduction type $I_1$ at
  $571$; the curve $2045b1$ has types $I_{17}$ at $5$ (nonsplit) and $I_1$
  at $409$.  All Tamagawa numbers are $1$, and both torsion groups are
  trivial.
\item \emph{$2$-descent.}  A $2$-descent in PARI/GP, with the class
  groups of the descent fields fully certified (so independent of GRH),
  gives $\dim\Sha[2]=2$ for both curves.  For $571a1$ the Cassels pairing
  is nondegenerate on $\Sha[2]$.  If $x\in\Sha[4]$ then
  $\langle 2x,y\rangle=\langle x,2y\rangle=0$ for all $y\in\Sha[2]$, so
  $2x=0$; repeating the argument gives $\Sha[2^\infty]=\Sha[2]\cong
  H_2$.  For $2045b1$ the descent shows $\Sha[2]=2\,\Sha[4]$; choosing a
  half of each element of $\Sha[2]$ gives a bijection
  $\Sha[2]\times\Sha[2]\to\Sha[4]$, so $\#\Sha[4]=16$.
\item \emph{Eight-descent.}  For $2045b1$ we use the published
  eight-descent computation in the proof of
  \citet[Theorem~7.1]{Miller2011}, which applies to optimal curves of
  conductor below $5000$ whose analytic $2$-primary prediction has
  valuation $4$; it gives $\Sha[8]=\Sha[4]$.  Hence
  $\Sha[2^\infty]=\Sha[4]$, a group of order $16$ and $2$-rank $2$,
  that is, $H_4$.  We use this intermediate computation, not the BSD
  formula.
\item \emph{Whole orders.}  Since $\Sha$ is finite, $\#\Sha=4u$ for
  $571a1$ and $16v$ for $2045b1$ with $u,v$ odd.  These differ, because
  $4u\equiv4$ and $16v\equiv0\pmod 8$; the odd parts are not needed.
\end{enumerate}
Lean checks the group-theoretic steps: the collapse
$\Sha[2^\infty]=\Sha[2]$ from nondegeneracy, the halving bijection and
the resulting count of $\Sha[4]$, the stabilization from
$\Sha[8]=\Sha[4]$, the classification of perfect alternating pairings on
$(\Z/4\Z)^2$ (each is a unit multiple of the standard one), and the
congruence in step~5.  For $571a1$ it also checks the explicit
quartic-cover maps and the numerical local-solubility conditions of the
descent.  The descent and modular-symbol computations, the arithmetic
theorems in steps~1--4, and the published eight-descent are external
inputs.

\begin{obligation}[Finite-source transport]\label{obl:apparatus:rho-finite-transport}
The Bloch--Kato finite component requires a comparison
\[
  \beta_{\mathrm{BK,finite}}:
  L_{\mathrm{finite}}(E)_{\R}
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta_f(M_E)_{\mathrm{finite}}
\]
that fixes the integral local conditions at bad primes, the
squared-torsion normalization, and the determinant-line convention for
the $\Sha$ factor: an integral presentation of each paired group,
together with a generator and an orientation of the corresponding
determinant line.  The paired group determines the order $\#S_p$, but the
pairing does not canonically select a rational lift of its Pfaffian
coefficient, nor a prescribed generator or orientation of the determinant
line; these must come from the presentation.  Even an integral
presentation does not determine a prescribed generator from the
determinant ideal.  For the ordinary Selmer complex of the curve $1913b1$
at $p=3$, whose inverse determinant ideal in $\Z_3$ is $(9)$, the
elements $9$ and $18$ both generate that ideal, but only $9$ is a square
in $\Q_3$; the construction is described in the companion
paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDClosure2026}.  This comparison is external
arithmetic input and is not derived from the finite calculations above.
\end{obligation}
 \section{Local \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-adic column}
\label{sec:p-adic}

The local $p$-adic column records local data at a prime $p$ before they
are contracted to a single local number.  At a good ordinary prime this
means keeping the ordinary filtration together with the finite local
condition and its logarithm.  At a good supersingular prime it means
keeping the signed $+$ and $-$ local conditions separately rather than
adding them.

\begin{definition}[Real $p$-adic map]\label{def:apparatus:p-adic-map}
Let $T=T_pE$ and $V=V_pE=T\otimes\Qp$, as representations of $G_{\Qp}$,
and suppose $E$ has good reduction at $p$.  The finite local
condition~\citep{BlochKato1990,Fontaine1994} is
\[
  \Hf(\Qp,V)
  =
  \ker\bigl(H^1(\Qp,V)\to H^1(\Qp,V\otimes B_{\mathrm{cris}})\bigr).
\]
Since $1$ is not an eigenvalue of Frobenius on $D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)$, the
Bloch--Kato exponential
$\exp_V:D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)/\operatorname{Fil}^0D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\to\Hf(\Qp,V)$
is an isomorphism of one-dimensional $\Qp$-vector spaces; its inverse is
the logarithm
\[
  \log_V:\Hf(\Qp,V)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)/\operatorname{Fil}^0D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V).
\]
If $E$ has good ordinary reduction~\citep{Mazur1972}, with ordinary
filtration $0\to T^+\to T\to T^-\to0$, the native ordinary family is
\[
  L_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(E)=\bigl(T^+,\ \Hf(\Qp,V),\ \log_V\bigr).
\]
If $p$ is odd and $E$ has good supersingular reduction with $a_p=0$, the
native signed family~\citep{Kobayashi2003,Pollack2003} is
\[
  L_p^{\pm}(E)=\bigl(H^1_+,\ H^1_-,\ \kappa_\pm\bigr),
\]
where $H^1_\pm$ are Kobayashi's plus and minus local conditions (at a
specified level of the cyclotomic tower, with specified coefficients) and
$\kappa_\pm$ are the corresponding plus and minus Coleman maps.  Other
supersingular cases, including $p=2$, are outside this definition.

These native families consist of $p$-adic objects.  A real local map
\[
  \rho_{p,\mathrm{ord}}^{\R}:L_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(E)\longrightarrow
  \Delta_f(V,T)_{p,\R}^{\mathrm{ord}},
  \qquad
  \rho_{p,\pm}^{\R}:L_p^{\pm}(E)\longrightarrow
  \Delta_f(V,T)_{p,\R}^{\pm},
\]
reading them in a real line, is \emph{not} constructed here.  It requires
a rational structure on the relevant $p$-adic determinant lines (for
example on the line of $\log_V$), and in the signed case a choice of
specialization or localization of the Iwasawa-theoretic Coleman maps
together with a normalization.  We treat these real readouts as supplied
data, part of the local transport of \Cref{obl:apparatus:rho-p-transport}.
The calculations below only concern which native coordinates such a
readout is allowed to see.
\end{definition}

The logarithm, not the dual exponential, is the right coordinate on the
finite condition.  Under the identification $V^*(1)\cong V$ given by the
Weil pairing, the dual exponential is a map
$\exp^*:H^1(\Qp,V)\to\operatorname{Fil}^0D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)$ with a
different target, and it vanishes on $\Hf(\Qp,V)$: by the reciprocity law
relating $\exp^*$ to $\exp$ under the local Tate pairing, and the
orthogonality of the finite conditions for $V$ and $V^*(1)$, every
pairing of $\exp^*(h)$ with $D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V^*(1))$ vanishes for
$h\in\Hf(\Qp,V)$, so $\exp^*(h)=0$~\citep{BlochKato1990,Kato2004}.
Restricted to the finite condition, the dual exponential therefore
carries no information.

\begin{theorem}[Ordinary local Schur collapse]\label{thm:apparatus:ordinary-schur-collapse}
For a good ordinary row, let $x=(x_{\mathrm{ord}},x_f,x_{\log})\in\R^3$ be
tangent coordinates for the ordinary filtration, the finite local
condition and the logarithm.  With
$D_{\mathrm{ord}}(x)=x$, $L_{\mathrm{ord,full}}(x)=x$ and $C=I_3$, one has
$\XiC=0$.  If the coordinate $x_{\mathrm{ord}}$ is omitted from the source,
the residual has trace $1$.\footnote{Lean proves the coordinate calculation.  Its formal ordinary
local map uses the logarithm, supplied together with its inverse
exponential as an explicit isomorphism, and Lean separately derives that
the dual exponential vanishes on finite classes from the reciprocity and
orthogonality laws.  Lean does not show that the three coordinates vary
independently for an actual curve, and does not construct the local
determinant comparison.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With the full source all blocks equal $I_3$, so $\XiC=I_3-I_3I_3I_3=0$.
Omitting $x_{\mathrm{ord}}$ leaves the source spanned by $(0,1,0)$ and
$(0,0,1)$, with projector $\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1)$; the residual
$\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)$ has trace $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Signed local Schur collapse]\label{thm:apparatus:signed-schur-collapse}
For a signed row as in \Cref{def:apparatus:p-adic-map} ($p$ odd, good
supersingular reduction, $a_p=0$), let $y=(y_+,y_-,y_{\kappa})\in\R^3$ be
tangent coordinates for the two signed conditions and the signed
comparison.  With $D_{\pm}(y)=y$, $L_{\pm,\mathrm{full}}(y)=y$ and
$C=I_3$, one has $\XiC=0$.  The unsigned source
$L_{\mathrm{uns}}(y)=(y_++y_-,\,y_{\kappa})$ has rank $2$, leaves the
direction $(1,-1,0)$ invisible, and has residual trace $1$.\footnote{Lean proves the coordinate calculation over $\Q$ and exhibits the
two signed inputs with the same unsigned image.  The signed comparison is
a separately supplied carrier; actual signed local conditions, regulators
and determinants are not constructed.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With the full source, $\XiC=0$ as in the ordinary case.  The unsigned
source is spanned by the orthogonal vectors $u_1=(1,1,0)$ and
$u_2=(0,0,1)$, so its projector is $P=u_1u_1^{\mathsf T}/2+u_2u_2^{\mathsf
T}$, of trace $2/2+1=2$, and $\tr(I_3-P)=1$.  The vector $(1,-1,0)$ is
orthogonal to $u_1$ and $u_2$; equivalently, the signed inputs
$(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$ have the same unsigned image $(1,0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{obligation}[Local $p$-adic transport]\label{obl:apparatus:rho-p-transport}
The Bloch--Kato local component requires a comparison
\[
  \beta_{\mathrm{BK},p}:
  \Delta_f(V,T)_{p,\R}
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta_f(M_E)_p
\]
together with the real readouts $\rho_{p,\mathrm{ord}}^{\R}$ and
$\rho_{p,\pm}^{\R}$ of \Cref{def:apparatus:p-adic-map}, fixing the
rational structures and normalizations of the $p$-adic determinant lines,
the integral Fontaine--Laffaille or Wach-module data, the
orientation of the logarithm and of the local Tate pairing~\citep{Milne1986},
local--global compatibility of Selmer complexes~\citep{Nekovar2006}, the
signed normalization at supersingular primes, and compatibility with the
determinant-assembly column.  This comparison is external arithmetic
input; the coordinate calculations above do not construct it.
\end{obligation}
 \section{Determinant-assembly column}
\label{sec:det-assembly}

The four arithmetic columns produce four factors.  To obtain an element of
one determinant line one also needs an orientation: the signs, units and
trivialization conventions under which the factors are multiplied.  The
determinant-assembly column holds this orientation as an independent
input.

\begin{definition}[Real determinant-assembly map]\label{def:apparatus:det-assembly-map}
Let
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{loc}}(E)
  =
  \rho_{\mathrm{ht/reg}}^{\R}
  \otimes
  \rho_{\mathrm{an/period}}^{\R}
  \otimes
  \rho_{\mathrm{finite}}^{\R}
  \otimes
  \bigotimes_{p\in S(E)}\rho_p^{\R}
\]
be the tensor of the four arithmetic columns over a finite support set
$S(E)$ of primes, with the local factors at primes outside $S(E)$
trivialized as units.  Let $o_{\det}\in C_{\det}(E)$ be an orientation and
trivialization datum, supplied independently of the four columns, and let
$\rho_{\det}^{\R}(o_{\det})\in\R^\times$ be the orientation scalar it
determines (the map on the orientation slot from
\Cref{def:apparatus:bk-component-map}).  The real determinant assembly is
\[
  \mathcal A^{\R}(E)
  =
  \lambda_{\mathrm{loc}}(E)\cdot\rho_{\det}^{\R}(o_{\det})
  \in\Delta_f(M_E)_{\R}.
\]
We call $\mathcal A^{\R}(E)$ the \emph{final assembly}.  It already
contains the four arithmetic factors, and so must not be used again as a
factor alongside them; see \Cref{def:apparatus:five-column-tensor}.
\end{definition}

The adequacy question for this column is whether the four arithmetic
columns, without the orientation, can explain the assembled target.  In
the coordinate model they cannot, and the shortfall is exactly one
direction.

\begin{theorem}[Cascade-completion Schur signature]\label{thm:apparatus:cascade-completion-signature}
Use the tangent coordinates
\[
  x=(x_{\mathrm{ht}},x_{\mathrm{an}},x_{\mathrm{fin}},x_p,x_{\det})\in\R^5 .
\]
Let the four arithmetic columns supply the source row
$\ell=(1,1,1,1,0)$ and let the assembled target have row
$d=(1,1,1,1,1)$.  With $C=I_5$,
\[
  \KDD=dd^{\mathsf T}=5,\qquad
  \KLL=\ell\ell^{\mathsf T}=4,\qquad
  \KDL=d\ell^{\mathsf T}=4,
  \qquad
  \XiC=5-4\cdot4^{-1}\cdot4=1.
\]
The residual is the orientation direction $e_{\det}=(0,0,0,0,1)$.\footnote{Lean proves these exact integer values.  The statement concerns
the orientation direction only; it does not assert adequacy of each of the
four arithmetic coordinates.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The three blocks are the displayed sums of squares and products of
entries, and the one-dimensional source block $4$ has inverse $4^{-1}$.
Hence $\XiC=5-4=1$.  Since $d=\ell+e_{\det}$ with $\ell\cdot e_{\det}=0$,
projecting $d$ onto $\ell$ leaves $e_{\det}$, of squared length $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{obligation}[Integral determinant assembly]\label{obl:apparatus:rho-det-assembly}
The full determinant comparison requires, for each orientation datum
$o_{\det}$, a transport
\[
  \beta_{\det\text{-}\mathrm{assembly}}(\,\cdot\,;o_{\det}):
  \Delta_{\mathrm{ht/reg}}
  \otimes
  \Delta_{\mathrm{an/period}}
  \otimes
  \Delta_{\mathrm{finite}}
  \otimes
  \bigotimes_{p\in S(E)}\Delta_p
  \longrightarrow
  \Delta_f(M_E),
\]
together with compatible unit trivializations of the local lines at primes
outside $S(E)$.  It must fix the Knudsen--Mumford determinant
signs~\citep{KnudsenMumford1976}, the local--global compatibility of
Selmer complexes, the convention relating the zeta element to the
motivic trivialization, and compatibility with the four column
transports.  The orientation datum is a parameter of the transport,
distinct from the assembled output.  This transport is external
arithmetic input.  The residual computation above shows only that, in the
coordinate model, the four arithmetic rows do not account for the
orientation direction; it does not show that the orientation varies
independently for actual curves.
\end{obligation}
 \section[Comparison with the Bloch--Kato scalar package]{Comparison with the Bloch--Kato scalar package}
\label{sec:comparison}

The preceding sections built the five columns separately.  This section
contracts them back to the usual Bloch--Kato scalar package and asks two
questions: is the typed source adequate for that contraction, and what
does the contraction forget?

\begin{remark}[Relation to the standard presentations]\label{rmk:apparatus:five-column-literature-gap}
The fundamental line and its Tamagawa-number formulation go back to
Bloch--Kato~\citep[\S5]{BlochKato1990} and
Fontaine--Perrin-Riou~\citep{FontainePerrinRiou1994}; the formulation of
the leading value in terms of periods and height determinants, modulo
$\Q^\times$, is surveyed by Nekov\'a\v{r}~\citep{Nekovar1994}.  The
five-slot organization used here is a bookkeeping choice made for the
adequacy tests.  We have not compared it systematically with the
literature and make no claim about whether equivalent decompositions
appear there.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Five-column tensor]\label{def:apparatus:five-column-tensor}
The typed five-column tensor is
\[
  T_{\mathrm{5col}}(E)
  =
  \rho_{\mathrm{ht/reg}}^{\R}(E)
  \otimes
  \rho_{\mathrm{an/period}}^{\R}(E)
  \otimes
  \rho_{\mathrm{finite}}^{\R}(E)
  \otimes
  \bigotimes_{p\in S(E)}\rho_p^{\R}(E)
  \otimes
  \rho_{\det}^{\R}(o_{\det}),
\]
whose fifth factor is the orientation scalar of the independent
orientation datum (\Cref{def:apparatus:det-assembly-map}), not the final
assembly $\mathcal A^{\R}(E)$.  Its source family consists of the five
separated logarithmic tangent coordinates
$(x_{\mathrm{ht}},x_{\mathrm{an}},x_{\mathrm{fin}},x_p,x_{\det})$, where
$x_{\det}=\log|\rho_{\det}^{\R}(o_{\det})|$.  The
\emph{orientation-normalized scalar readout} is
\[
  \log\bigl|T_{\mathrm{5col}}(E)\big/\rho_{\det}^{\R}(o_{\det})\bigr|
  =x_{\mathrm{ht}}+x_{\mathrm{an}}+x_{\mathrm{fin}}+x_p ,
\]
the logarithm of the absolute value of the product of the four arithmetic
factors.  It is distinct from the full contraction
$\log|T_{\mathrm{5col}}(E)|$, whose row is $(1,1,1,1,1)$.
\end{definition}

The distinction in the definition matters.  Since $\mathcal A^{\R}(E)$
already contains the four arithmetic factors, using it as the fifth factor
would count each of them twice.  For instance, with arithmetic factors
$2,3,5,7$ and orientation scalar $1$, the final assembly is $210$; the
tensor with the orientation as fifth factor is again $210$, whereas
reusing the final assembly would give $210^2=44100$.  (Lean checks these
values.)

\begin{theorem}[Five-column to Bloch--Kato scalar contraction]\label{thm:apparatus:five-col-bk-comparison}
In the coordinates
$x=(x_{\mathrm{ht}},x_{\mathrm{an}},x_{\mathrm{fin}},x_p,x_{\det})\in\R^5$,
the orientation-normalized scalar readout of
\Cref{def:apparatus:five-column-tensor} is
$\Phi_{\mathrm{5col}\to\mathrm{BK}}(x)=d\cdot x$ with $d=(1,1,1,1,0)$.
With the full typed source $L(x)=x$ and $C=I_5$,
\[
  \Xi_{\mathrm{5col}\to\mathrm{BK}}
  =
  \|d\|^2-d\,I_5\,d^{\mathsf T}
  =
  4-4=0,
\]
so the typed source is adequate for this readout.  The orientation
direction $e_{\det}=(0,0,0,0,1)$ lies in the kernel of
$\Phi_{\mathrm{5col}\to\mathrm{BK}}$: the readout forgets at least that
coordinate.\footnote{Lean proves the contraction row, the zero residual and the
kernel vector.  Identifying this coordinate readout with the arithmetic
Bloch--Kato scalar requires the five transports of the column sections
and, in addition, a convention under which that scalar is independent of
the orientation datum.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With the identity source, $\KLL=\Kdagger=I_5$, $\KDL=d$ and
$K_{LD}=d^{\mathsf T}$, so the projected energy is
$d\,I_5\,d^{\mathsf T}=dd^{\mathsf T}=4$, which equals $\KDD$; hence the
residual is $0$.  Finally $d\cdot e_{\det}=0$, so changing only
$x_{\det}$ changes the typed coordinate without changing the scalar
readout.
\end{proof}

The two halves of the theorem fit together.  The orientation-normalized
readout is computed correctly from the typed source, so nothing is lost
in that direction; but the typed source carries an orientation coordinate
that this readout does not see, while the full tensor does (changing the
orientation scalar from $1$ to $2$ in the example above changes the
tensor from $210$ to $420$).  To the extent that the arithmetic
Bloch--Kato scalar is orientation-normalized in this sense, which is an
additional convention not supplied by the transports alone, it is a
projection of the typed atlas.
 \section{Rank two: the height matrix and its sources}
\label{sec:higher-rank-no-go}

In rank at least $2$ the height/regulator column raises a question that
does not arise in rank $1$.  Once a Mordell--Weil basis is known, the
N\'eron--Tate Gram matrix computes the regulator.  But a theory that
explains the regulator through independent cycle classes (generalized
Gross--Zagier~\citep{GrossZagier1986}, Beilinson--Kato~\citep{Beilinson1985,Kato2004},
$K_2(E)$ or higher Chow groups~\citep{Bloch1986}) would need more than the
Gram matrix records.  This section makes the rank-two height calculation
explicit and then states, in the simplest form, what the Gram matrix
forgets.

\begin{definition}[Rank-two Mordell--Weil/Kummer source]\label{def:apparatus:rank-two-kummer-source}
Let $\Lambda_E=E(\Q)/E(\Q)_{\tors}$ and, for a prime $p$, let
\[
  \kappa_p:\Lambda_E\otimes\Qp\longrightarrow \Hf(\Q,V_pE)
\]
be the Kummer map into the Bloch--Kato Selmer group.  For a curve of rank
$2$ with a fixed Mordell--Weil basis $(P_1,P_2)$, the rank-two
Mordell--Weil/Kummer source is the pair
$\bigl(\kappa_p(P_1),\kappa_p(P_2)\bigr)$.  It is a classical Selmer source
once the basis is known; it is not, by this definition, a construction of
Heegner cycles or other motivic classes.
\end{definition}

For the curve $389a1$, $y^2+y=x^3+x^2-2x$, of rank $2$, take
$P_1=(0,0)$ and $P_2=(1,0)$.  Computing N\'eron--Tate heights with
PARI/GP~\citep{PARI2023} gives the Gram matrix
\[
  H=\begin{pmatrix}0.3270008\ldots & 0.0585227\ldots\\
  0.0585227\ldots & 0.4767117\ldots\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \det H=0.1524602\ldots .
\]
This determinant equals the regulator recorded in the LMFDB~\citep{LMFDB}.
Since a subgroup of index $m$ in $\Lambda_E$ has Gram determinant
$m^2\Reg(E/\Q)$, the points $P_1,P_2$ form a basis of $\Lambda_E$.  By
\Cref{thm:apparatus:height-schur-collapse}, the row of
$d(\log\det)_H$ in the coordinates
$(\delta h_{11},\delta h_{12},\delta h_{22})$ is
\[
  \ell=\Bigl(\frac{h_{22}}{\det H},\,-\frac{2h_{12}}{\det H},\,
  \frac{h_{11}}{\det H}\Bigr)
  =(3.12679\ldots,\,-0.76771\ldots,\,2.14483\ldots),
  \qquad
  \|\ell\|^2=14.96650\ldots .
\]

\begin{theorem}[Rank-two height Schur collapse]\label{thm:apparatus:rank-two-height-schur-collapse}
For the row $\ell$ above, with the full symmetric height source and
$C=I_3$,
\[
  \Xi_{\mathrm{NT}\to\Reg}=\|\ell\|^2-\ell\,I_3\,\ell^{\mathsf T}=0 .
\]
The source consisting of the single direction $\ell$ (the
``determinant-only'' source) has projector of trace $1$ and leaves a
residual of trace $3-1=2$.\footnote{Lean proves the statement for the integer row
$(312679,-76771,214483)$, which is $10^5\ell$ rounded to integers, and
proves the trace-one projector onto that scaled direction over $\Q$.  The
general rank-two row formula is proved separately
(\Cref{thm:apparatus:height-schur-collapse}); the numerical heights of
$389a1$ are computed outside Lean.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With the full source, $\KLL=\Kdagger=I_3$ and
$\ell\,I_3\,\ell^{\mathsf T}=\|\ell\|^2=\KDD$, so the residual is $0$.  The
orthogonal projector onto the line spanned by $\ell\neq0$ is
$P_\ell=\ell^{\mathsf T}\ell/\|\ell\|^2$, of trace $1$, so
$\tr(I_3-P_\ell)=2$.
\end{proof}

The determinant is therefore an adequate readout of itself, but it does
not retain the three-dimensional height matrix.  The next statement goes
one step further: even the full height matrix does not retain data that a
cycle-theoretic explanation of the regulator would have to supply.

\begin{theorem}[The Gram matrix forgets provenance data]\label{thm:apparatus:gram-matrix-shadow}
Fix $E/\Q$ of Mordell--Weil rank $r\ge2$ and a set $\mathcal P$ of
provenance data with at least two elements (for example, records of which
cycle construction is claimed to produce each basis class).  Define a
\emph{cycle-source record} to be a Mordell--Weil basis
$(P_1,\ldots,P_r)$ of $\Lambda_E$ together with an element of
$\mathcal P$.
The map sending a record to its Gram matrix
$\bigl(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}\bigr)_{i,j}$ is not injective.
In particular, the Gram matrix, which determines
$\Reg(E/\Q)=\det(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}})$, does not determine
the provenance data.\footnote{Lean proves a narrower record-model statement: the projection
from records consisting of an integer Gram identifier and three Boolean
metadata fields to the identifier is not injective.  It constructs no
Mordell--Weil bases, height matrices or cycle classes.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $r=\rank E(\Q)$, a basis exists.  Fix one and attach to it two
distinct elements of $\mathcal P$.  The two records are distinct and have
the same Gram matrix, because the Gram matrix depends only on the
points.
\end{proof}

The theorem is deliberately weak: it says only that the height matrix,
which is a function of the points, cannot certify anything about where
those points come from.  It does not rule out cycle constructions such as
those of Bertolini--Darmon--Prasanna~\citep{BertoliniDarmonPrasanna2013},
Beilinson--Kato, or higher Chow groups, which bring their own input.  In
the companion paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDClosure2026} the rank-$\ge2$
leading-term identity enters as a supplied hypothesis, not as a theorem.
 \section{Finite windows of curves}
\label{sec:global-audit-no-go}

Numerical checks of BSD-type statements are made on finite lists of
curves.  This section records the elementary but easily forgotten fact
that a statement about all curves cannot be decided from a fixed finite
list.  The list used for examples in this paper is the five-curve window
\[
  W=\{11a1,\ 37a1,\ 121b1,\ 960d1,\ 571a1\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[A finite window does not decide a universal statement]\label{thm:apparatus:finite-window-no-go}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
$\Q$, and consider assignments $\tau:\mathcal C\to\{\mathrm{true},
\mathrm{false}\}$, thought of as truth values of some property of curves.
Let $U(\tau)$ be the statement ``$\tau(E)=\mathrm{true}$ for every $E$''.
\begin{enumerate}
\item No function of the restriction $\tau|_W$ computes $U(\tau)$ for all
  assignments $\tau$: the assignment $\tau_1\equiv\mathrm{true}$ and the
  assignment $\tau_2$ that agrees with $\tau_1$ except at one curve
  $E_*\notin W$, where it is false, have the same restriction to $W$, but
  $U(\tau_1)$ is true and $U(\tau_2)$ is false.
\item In Schur form, let $\mathcal C(B)$ be the finite set of curves of
  conductor at most $B\ge960$, with $M(B)=\#\mathcal C(B)$ coordinates.
  The window source is the coordinate projection onto the five
  coordinates in $W$, and the residual against the identity readout on
  $\mathcal C(B)$ has trace $M(B)-5$, which tends to infinity with $B$.\footnote{Lean proves both parts for a model in which curves are indexed
by natural numbers and the window is the first five indices: the two
completions agree on the window, differ outside it, are separated by the
universal target, and are identified by every function of the window;
and the residual trace $M-5$ takes every natural-number value.  The model
says nothing about which truth assignments arise from actual properties
of curves.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) Any function of $\tau|_W$ takes the same value on $\tau_1$ and
$\tau_2$, while $U$ separates them.  (2) The trace of a coordinate
projection is the number of retained coordinates, so
$\tr(I_{M(B)}-P_W)=M(B)-5$.  There are infinitely many isomorphism classes
of elliptic curves over $\Q$ and finitely many of each conductor, so
$M(B)\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

The point of the statement is methodological.  A conclusion about all
curves needs an argument that reaches the curves outside the window, for
instance a theorem covering a family, or an exhaustion argument, or a
passage from density statements to pointwise ones.  The finite window by
itself supplies none of these.
 \section{Finite sets of primes}
\label{sec:support-prime-no-go}

The local columns are indexed by primes.  This section records the local
analogue of the previous one: a fixed finite set of primes cannot certify
a statement about all primes.  It then describes the numerical support
table used for the examples, and says precisely what that table does and
does not establish.

\begin{theorem}[A finite set of primes does not decide all-prime coverage]\label{thm:apparatus:finite-prime-cover-no-go}
Let a \emph{coverage record} assign to each prime $p$ a status, covered or
uncovered, and let the all-prime target be the statement that every prime
is covered.  For every finite set $P_0$ of primes there is a prime
$q\notin P_0$, and there are two coverage records that agree at every
prime of $P_0$, such that the first covers every prime and the second
leaves $q$ uncovered.  Consequently no function of the restriction of a
record to $P_0$ decides the all-prime target.\footnote{Lean proves the statement for coverage records indexed by natural
numbers, with an explicit prime $q$ dividing $1+\prod_{p\in P_0}p$, and
with primality decided by a finite divisor check.  These are formal
records; no claim is made that they arise from elliptic curves.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $q$ be a prime factor of $1+\prod_{p\in P_0}p$; then $q\notin P_0$.
Let the first record cover every prime and let the second agree with it
except at $q$.  The two records have the same restriction to $P_0$, so
any function of that restriction gives them the same value, while the
all-prime target holds for the first and fails for the second.
\end{proof}

For computations one works with a finite truncation of the local column.

\begin{definition}[Support-prime truncation residual]\label{def:apparatus:support-prime-truncation-residual}
Fix a curve $E$ and a set $R(E)$ of primes, the \emph{residual rows}: the
primes at which no local comparison is taken as available.  For $B\ge1$
the truncation residual is
\[
  \Xi_B(E)=\#\{p\le B:\ p\text{ prime},\ p\in R(E)\}.
\]
It depends only on $R(E)$ below $B$.  In the examples below $R(E)$ is the
set of primes of bad reduction.  For the five window curves and $B=49$
this gives
\[
  \Xi_{49}(11a1)=\Xi_{49}(37a1)=\Xi_{49}(121b1)=1,\qquad
  \Xi_{49}(960d1)=3,\qquad
  \Xi_{49}(571a1)=0,
\]
the last becoming $1$ once $B\ge571$.  This is a finite diagnostic,
distinct from \Cref{thm:apparatus:finite-prime-cover-no-go}.
\end{definition}

Taking $R(E)$ to be the bad primes treats every good prime as covered by
an ordinary or signed local theory.  For good ordinary primes this is the
setting of \Cref{thm:apparatus:ordinary-schur-collapse}.  For good
supersingular primes, the signed theory of
\Cref{def:apparatus:p-adic-map} requires $p$ odd and $a_p=0$, and the
table below shows that this fails in some small cases.

  \section{A formal source algebra in rank two}
\label{sec:vsrc}

The rank-two discussion of \Cref{sec:higher-rank-no-go} suggests separating
two questions: what algebraic shape a rank-$r$ source should have, and
whether actual cycle classes realize that shape.  This section treats the
first question with the simplest possible device, the exterior algebra on
$r$ formal symbols.  The top product $J_1\cdots J_r$ plays the role of a
determinant: it is nonzero exactly when the symbols are independent.  We
call this algebra the \emph{virtual source algebra} $\vsrc_r$; the word
``virtual'' signals that the symbols are not, by themselves, cycle classes.

\begin{definition}[Virtual source algebra]\label{def:apparatus:vsrc-algebra}
Let $V_r=\Q J_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\Q J_r$ and $\vsrc_r=\Lambda_{\Q}(V_r)$,
the exterior algebra.  In rank $2$ it has ordered basis
$1,\ \Jone,\ \Jtwo,\ \Jtwelve:=\Jone\Jtwo$ and relations
\[
  \Jone^2=0,\qquad \Jtwo^2=0,\qquad
  \Jone\Jtwo=\Jtwelve,\qquad
  \Jtwo\Jone=-\Jtwelve.
\]
The element $\Jtwelve$ is the determinant-shaped witness of independence
of $\Jone$ and $\Jtwo$.\footnote{Lean constructs the rank-two multiplication table on integer
coordinates and proves associativity, the unit laws and additive and
scalar bilinearity.  Rational coefficients, other ranks and the dimension
count are treated here on paper.}
\end{definition}

\Cref{fig:vsrc-wedge} displays the rank-two algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Stage I: consistency]\label{thm:apparatus:vsrc-stage-i-consistency}
$\vsrc_2$ is an associative, unital, graded-commutative $\Q$-algebra.
Every element has a unique normal form
$a_{\varnothing}1+a_1\Jone+a_2\Jtwo+a_{12}\Jtwelve$ with $a_\bullet\in\Q$,
so $\dim_{\Q}\vsrc_2=4$.  In lower rank, $\vsrc_0=\Q$ and
$\vsrc_1=\Q\oplus\Q J_1$ with $J_1^2=0$.\footnote{Lean proves the unit laws, the four relations and the
distinctness of the basis symbols for the integer-coordinate model, and
records the numbers $2^0,2^1,2^2$; it does not prove the dimension
statement over $\Q$.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take the $\Q$-vector space with basis indexed by the subsets of
$\{1,2\}$.  Define the product of two basis vectors to be $0$ if the
subsets meet, and otherwise to be the basis vector of the union times the
sign of the permutation that sorts the concatenated index list; extend
bilinearly.  This gives the displayed relations, and the empty subset is a
unit.  Associativity holds on basis vectors because both
parenthesizations vanish when an index repeats, and otherwise both
produce the same union with the sign of the same sorting permutation.
Moving a degree-$a$ monomial past a degree-$b$ monomial costs
$(-1)^{ab}$, which is graded commutativity.  The four subsets give a basis,
so the dimension is $4$; the same construction with $\{1\}$ or
$\varnothing$ gives $\vsrc_1$ and $\vsrc_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No rank-$r$ realization is supplied here]\label{rmk:apparatus:vsrc-no-licensed-descent}
For $r\ge2$, nothing in this paper provides a map
\[
  \vsrc_r(E)\longrightarrow \mathrm{CH}^*(\mathcal X)_{\Q},
\]
or into a Selmer or Mordell--Weil group, that realizes $J_1,\ldots,J_r$ as
independent cycle classes.  The Gram-matrix statement
\Cref{thm:apparatus:gram-matrix-shadow} shows that heights alone do not
supply such provenance.  Constructions of cycle classes with arithmetic
meaning, such as Kato's Euler system~\citep{Kato2004} and generalized
Heegner cycles~\citep{BertoliniDarmonPrasanna2013}, come with their own
hypotheses; we do not use them, and we make no claim about whether they
realize $\vsrc_r$ for a given curve.
\end{remark}

In rank $1$ the situation is classical.  Under the Gross--Zagier
hypotheses~\citep{GrossZagier1986,Kolyvagin1990} for an imaginary
quadratic field $K$, there is a Heegner point $P_K\in E(K)$, and one can
send $J_1$ to $P_K$.

\begin{theorem}[Stage II: rank one]\label{thm:apparatus:vsrc-stage-ii-gz-partial}
Send $J_1\mapsto P_K$ and the formal height $h_{\vsrc}(J_1,J_1)$ to the
N\'eron--Tate height $\langle P_K,P_K\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The $1\times1$ Gram determinant of the image source is
  $\langle P_K,P_K\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}$.  If $P_K$ has infinite order and
  $E(K)$ has rank $1$, this equals $[\Lambda:\Z P_K]^2\Reg(E/K)$, where
  $\Lambda=E(K)/E(K)_{\tors}$ and $[\Lambda:\Z P_K]$ is the index of the
  image of $\Z P_K$.
\item The Gross--Zagier identity relating $L'(E/K,1)$ to
  $\langle P_K,P_K\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}$ is not determined by these
  symbolic data: two formal data sets can share $P_K$ and
  $\langle P_K,P_K\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}$ while having different analytic
  quotients.  Relative to the symbolic calculus the analytic identity is an
  additional input, which we record by a nonzero residual
  $\Xi_{\mathrm{GZ}}$.\footnote{Lean checks the symbolic assignments $J_1\mapsto P_K$ and
$h_{\vsrc}(J_1,J_1)\mapsto$ (N\'eron--Tate height), and records the
declared residual value $\Xi_{\mathrm{GZ}}=1$; it constructs neither the
Heegner point nor any height.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) The Gram determinant of a single vector is its height.  If $Q$
generates $\Lambda$ and $P_K\equiv mQ$ modulo torsion, then
$\langle P_K,P_K\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}=m^2\langle Q,Q\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}
=m^2\Reg(E/K)$ with $m=[\Lambda:\Z P_K]$.  (2) The symbolic data contain
neither $L'(E/K,1)$ nor the period, so they cannot constrain the analytic
side; the identity is the content of the Gross--Zagier theorem.
\end{proof}

In rank $2$ the corresponding statement is a one-way translation.  For
$389a1$ the Tamagawa product and the torsion order are $1$; we write
\[
  \Xi_{\mathrm{Sc}}
  =A_E-\Omega_E^+\,\Reg(E/\Q)\,\#\Sha(E/\Q)
\]
for the scalar BSD residual, assuming $\Sha(E/\Q)$ finite, which is not
known for this curve.  A \emph{rank-two realization} consists of a
$\Q$-vector space $W$ of classes (for instance cycle classes modulo
homological equivalence), a linear map $f:V_2\to W$, a symmetric
bilinear height pairing on $W$, and a bridge
$\mathcal B^{(2)}_{\mathrm{an\text{-}ht}}$ identifying $A_E$ with
$\Omega_E^+\#\Sha$ times the Gram determinant of $(f(\Jone),f(\Jtwo))$
for that pairing.  Put $r_2=\Lambda f:\vsrc_2\to\Lambda_{\Q}(W)$; then
$z_{12}=r_2(\Jtwelve)=f(\Jone)\wedge f(\Jtwo)$, and $z_{12}\neq0$ exactly
when $f(\Jone),f(\Jtwo)$ are linearly independent.  Let
$\Xi_{\mathrm{Mot}}$ be a \emph{motivic residual}, which by definition
vanishes exactly when a rank-two realization with $z_{12}\neq0$ is given.
All of these data are supplied; none is constructed here.

\begin{theorem}[Stage III: one-way translation for $389a1$]\label{thm:apparatus:vsrc-stage-iii-translation}
For the fixed pair $(\Xi_{\mathrm{Mot}},\Xi_{\mathrm{Sc}})$ just defined,
suppose given the analytic--height bridge in the form of the implication
$\Xi_{\mathrm{Mot}}=0\Rightarrow\Xi_{\mathrm{Sc}}=0$.  Then a rank-two
realization yields the scalar identity
$A_E=\Omega_E^+\Reg(E/\Q)\#\Sha(E/\Q)$.  The reverse implication
$\Xi_{\mathrm{Sc}}=0\Rightarrow\Xi_{\mathrm{Mot}}=0$ is not provided by
the scalar data; it holds only if supplied as separate content.\footnote{The formalization records this as a conditional schema: for a
fixed pair of residuals, the forward implication is projected from the
supplied bridge, and the reverse from supplied external content.  Lean
does not construct a realization or a bridge.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forward statement is the supplied implication.  No reverse construction of a realization or
of an independence certificate from the scalar equality is supplied
here.  (\Cref{thm:apparatus:gram-matrix-shadow} establishes only that
provenance data cannot be recovered from the Gram matrix in its record
model; it does not exclude a separately defined construction.)
\end{proof}

The statement must be read for the fixed pair.  A universal version,
asserting $\Xi_{\mathrm{Mot}}=0\Rightarrow\Xi_{\mathrm{Sc}}=0$ for all
values of the two residuals, would be false, for instance at
$(\Xi_{\mathrm{Mot}},\Xi_{\mathrm{Sc}})=(0,1)$.

\begin{remark}[The source reading carries more data]\label{rmk:apparatus:vsrc-rank-r-stronger}
The scalar leading-coefficient formula records a number.  A rank-$r$
source statement in the sense above also asks for a nonzero independent
wedge $J_1\wedge\cdots\wedge J_r$ realized by actual classes.  In this
sense the source reading carries more data than the scalar equality.  We
do not claim a general theorem that scalar BSD is strictly weaker than any
such source statement; the comparison above is for the fixed pair and the
supplied bridge.
\end{remark}
 \section[The normalization constant kappa-r]{The normalization constant $\kapr$}
\label{sec:kappa-normalization}

Some formulations of the leading term, including the one used in the
companion paper, write the arithmetic side as
$\kapr(E)\,\Omega_E\Reg_r(E)\,c_E$ with an unspecified constant
$\kapr(E)$.  This section identifies the constant for which that form is
the standard Strong BSD formula, and makes the hypotheses of that
identification explicit.  It is a comparison of two ways of writing the
same conjectural identity, not a derivation of either.

Throughout, $\Sha(E/\Q)$ is assumed finite, $\Omega_E$ is the BSD real
period, $\Reg_r(E)$ is the regulator ($1$ in rank $0$), $\Tam(E)=\prod_\ell
c_\ell(E)$, and
\[
  C=\Omega_E\,\Reg_r(E)\,\Tam(E),
  \qquad
  d=\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Normalization of $\kapr$]\label{thm:apparatus:kappa-r-normalization-equivalence}
Fix the convention $c_E=\Tam(E)$ and work in a field containing all the
quantities above (for curves, $\R$).  Assume $C\neq0$ and $d\neq0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The right-hand side of the cascade form
  \[
    \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}=\kapr(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Reg_r(E)\,c_E
  \]
  equals the right-hand side of the standard Strong BSD form
  \[
    \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
    =\frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)\,\Reg_r(E)\,\Omega_E\,\Tam(E)}{d}
  \]
  if and only if
  \[
    \kapr(E)=\frac{\#\Sha(E/\Q)}{\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}}
    \qquad\Bigl(\text{equivalently, } \kapr(E)\,d=\#\Sha(E/\Q)\Bigr).
  \]
\item If this normalization holds, the two forms are equivalent for every
  value of the left-hand side.  Conversely, if the two forms are
  equivalent for every value of the left-hand side, the normalization
  holds.
\end{enumerate}
For actual curves the hypotheses hold: $\Omega_E>0$, the regulator is
positive because the N\'eron--Tate pairing is positive definite on
$E(\Q)\otimes\R$, $\Tam(E)$ is a positive integer, and $d\ge1$.\footnote{The declaration recorded for this theorem is Lean's cancellation
of a nonzero common factor over the integers.  Additional Lean
declarations prove (1) and (2) over an arbitrary field, including the
reordering of the standard product and the cross-multiplied form, which
requires $d\neq0$.  Positivity of the period and the regulator for actual
curves is not formalized.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The standard right-hand side equals $(\#\Sha/d)\,C$, by commutativity and
the definition of division.  The cascade right-hand side is
$\kapr(E)\,C$.  Since $C\neq0$, $\kapr(E)\,C=(\#\Sha/d)\,C$ if and only if
$\kapr(E)=\#\Sha/d$; since $d\neq0$, this holds if and only if
$\kapr(E)\,d=\#\Sha$.  This proves (1).  For (2), equal right-hand sides
make the two equations equivalent for every left-hand side.  Conversely,
evaluate both forms at the left-hand side $\kapr(E)\,C$: the cascade form
holds there, so the standard form does too, and (1) gives the
normalization.
\end{proof}

Part (2) needs the quantifier over all values of the left-hand side.
Equivalence of the two equations at a single, fixed value says nothing:
for instance the equations $0=1$ and $0=2$ are both false, hence
logically equivalent, although $1\neq2$.  When we say below that the
cascade form ``agrees'' with the standard form we mean that their
right-hand sides are equal, as in (1).

The conditional closure of the companion paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDClosure2026}
uses this normalization to read its rank-$\ge2$ hypothesis as the
standard Strong BSD formula.
 \section{Scope}\label{sec:scope}


We collect here what the paper does not claim.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{No result about the truth of BSD.}  The paper proves neither
  BSD, nor the Bloch--Kato conjecture, nor an Iwasawa main conjecture,
  nor a Gross--Zagier formula.  All statements about adequacy and
  information loss concern specified readouts.
\item \emph{``Does not determine'' is not ``does not exist''.}  Each
  result of that form (\Cref{tab:no-go-suite}) says that a particular
  readout, on a particular domain, does not determine a particular target.
  None says that richer data, or a future theorem, cannot recover the
  target.  Several of these statements are proved on formal models (records
  with tags, truth assignments, coverage records); where an arithmetic
  realization is available, as for $\Sha[2]$ in \Cref{ex:apparatus:sha-pair},
  it is stated separately with its external inputs.
\item \emph{Finite coordinate calculations.}  The adequacy collapses are
  finite linear-algebra computations in declared coordinates
  (identity-matrix actions and projector traces).  They are not
  statements about operators on infinite-dimensional spaces, and they do
  not show that the declared coordinates vary independently for actual
  curves.
\item \emph{The five transports are inputs.}  The comparisons from the five
  native columns to the Bloch--Kato line, including the real readouts of
  the $p$-adic data and the orientation convention, are external
  arithmetic inputs, recorded as obligations in the column sections.
\item \emph{The source algebra is formal.}  The algebra $\vsrc_r$ fixes the
  shape of a rank-$r$ source; it is not a construction of cycle classes.
\end{enumerate}
 \section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}


The standard BSD formula is a product of scalar factors: an analytic
leading coefficient divided by a period, a regulator, and a finite factor
built from Tamagawa numbers, torsion and $\Sha$, with local $p$-adic
normalizations and determinant conventions hidden in the assembly.  The
formula is a powerful summary, but each factor is the image of richer
data.  The question of this paper is which of those data a given readout
retains.

The Schur residual $\XiC$ turns that question into finite linear algebra.
If all native coordinates of a column are admitted, the residual vanishes.
In general, for the declared target, the residual measures the variation
of the target that the admitted source coordinates do not capture; a
source that discards some coordinates can still be adequate for a target
that ignores them, as the orientation-normalized readout of
\Cref{thm:apparatus:five-col-bk-comparison} shows.  Most of the adequacy
collapses in this paper are elementary for exactly this reason.  Their
value is not depth but bookkeeping: they say which coordinates must be
present before a datum can serve as a source for a given readout.

The ``does not determine'' results complement the collapses.  Some are
formal (\Cref{thm:apparatus:finite-scalar-public-shadow,thm:apparatus:gram-matrix-shadow,thm:apparatus:finite-window-no-go,thm:apparatus:finite-prime-cover-no-go}),
and their content is the precise identification of what is forgotten.
One is arithmetic: the curves $571a1$ and $2045b1$
(\Cref{ex:apparatus:sha-pair}) have the same rank, the same Tamagawa
numbers, the same torsion and the same $2$-torsion dimension of $\Sha$,
but $2$-primary parts of $\Sha$ of orders $4$ and $16$.  The dimension of
$\Sha[2]$ is the datum most directly computed by $2$-descent, and the
example shows concretely that it is not the datum the BSD formula needs.

Three normalization points recur and deserve emphasis.  First, a
Cassels--Tate pairing with values in $\Q/\Z$ does not give a canonical
numerical Pfaffian.  The invariant finite factor is the order of the
paired group; an alternating integral presentation $A$ gives
$\#S_p=|\det A|=\Pf(A)^2$, which determines $|\Pf(A)|$ but not its sign
(\Cref{sec:finite-source}).  Second, on the finite
local condition the relevant map is the logarithm; the dual exponential
vanishes there (\Cref{sec:p-adic}).  Third, the orientation enters the
determinant assembly as an independent input; the assembled value must
not be reused as a factor (\Cref{sec:det-assembly,sec:comparison}).  Each
of these is a small point, but each changes a formula.

At the two ends of the apparatus sit two calibrations.  At the source end,
the exterior algebra $\vsrc_r$ records the shape that a rank-$r$ source
must have, and the rank-two discussion shows that a scalar leading-term
identity does not by itself provide such a source.  At the target end,
the normalization $\kapr=\#\Sha/\#E(\Q)_{\tors}^{\,2}$ is exactly what
makes the cascade form of the leading term coincide with the standard
one.

The language of typed columns, readouts and residuals comes from the Six
Birds framework~\citep{TsiokosNeedles2026,TsiokosFoundationsII2026,TsiokosFoundationsIII2026},
but nothing in the paper depends on that framework beyond the definitions
given here.  The apparatus is used by the companion
paper~\citep{TsiokosBSDClosure2026}, which treats the scalar BSD identity
as a closure problem under explicit hypotheses.
 \section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}


We separated the data behind the BSD formula into five columns and tested
each column with a finite Schur-complement residual.  The full native data
of each column are adequate for its readout, and the five columns together
are adequate for the orientation-normalized Bloch--Kato scalar, which
forgets the orientation coordinate.  Coarser readouts lose information in
precisely identified ways, and for the $2$-torsion dimension of $\Sha$
this loss occurs for actual curves.  The comparisons from the native
columns to the Bloch--Kato line remain external inputs.  The resulting
picture is a checklist: for each factor of the BSD formula, which source
data must be kept, and which normalizations must be fixed, before the
factor can be compared across theories.

\section{Lean formalization and supporting data}\label{app:formalization}

\subsection*{What is formalized}

The Lean~4 development accompanying this paper imports no external
mathematics library.  Its theorems depend only on Lean's standard axioms
(propositional extensionality, quotient soundness and classical choice,
together with their standard consequences); there are no project-specific
axioms, and no declaration is left unproved.  The formalized content is
finite and algebraic: coordinate calculations with explicit matrices,
finite groups with explicit pairings, and records with tags.  None of the
arithmetic geometry used in the paper is formalized; where a statement
depends on it, the footnote and the body say so.

Each of the $41$ labelled definitions, theorems, obligations, remarks,
warnings and nonclaims falls into one of five classes, used in the
footnotes and in \Cref{tab:formalization-coverage}.  (The example in
\Cref{sec:finite-source} is not one of these statements; its inputs are
described in the text.)
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Lean proves} (7 theorems): the Lean statement has the same
  mathematical content as the theorem in the body.
\item \emph{Model version} (11 theorems): Lean proves a narrower statement,
  for instance on a model of the objects (integer coordinates, records
  with tags, a rank-two case), and the footnote says what is narrower.  The
  general statement is proved in the body.
\item \emph{Conditional schema} (1 theorem, \Cref{thm:apparatus:vsrc-stage-iii-translation}):
  the conclusion is obtained by projecting a supplied hypothesis.  Lean
  checks the logical bookkeeping, not the hypothesis.
\item \emph{Lean definition} (11 definitions): the definition has a Lean
  counterpart.  Most of these are interfaces that take the relevant
  arithmetic operations as inputs rather than constructing them.
\item \emph{Not formalized} (11 statements): the five transport
  obligations, four remarks, one warning and one nonclaim.
\end{itemize}
These classes follow the coverage records in the repository, which compare
each Lean statement with the corresponding statement of the paper.

\subsection*{Supporting computations}

Some statements use computations outside Lean.  The $2$-descents,
modular symbols and local data for $571a1$, $1309a1$ and $2045b1$
(\Cref{ex:apparatus:sha-pair}) were computed with
PARI/GP~\citep{PARI2023}.  The heights of $389a1$
(\Cref{sec:higher-rank-no-go}) are the output of the PARI/GP command
\begin{center}
\texttt{ellheightmatrix(ellinit([0,1,1,-2,0]), [[0,0],[1,0]])}.
\end{center}
The
$76$-row table of \Cref{rmk:apparatus:support-prime-atlas} was computed by
a separate program that counts points of each reduction twice, by
exhaustive enumeration and by counting roots of quadratics.  The
$2$-primary computation for $2045b1$ also uses the published
eight-descent of \citet[proof of Theorem~7.1]{Miller2011}, which we did
not rerun.

\section{Version history}\label{app:version-history}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em, style=nextline]
\item[v1, 31 August 2026] First public version.
\item[v2, 4 October 2026] Revised after a statement-by-statement audit
  of the mathematics and of its Lean formalization.  Changes:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The $\Sha$ factor of the finite column is the order of the paired
    group, read off an integral presentation; the numerical Pfaffian of a
    $\Q/\Z$-valued pairing is not used.  The $\dim\Sha[2]$ result is
    proved for actual paired groups, and an arithmetic example
    ($571a1$ and $2045b1$) is added.
  \item The finite local coordinate is the Bloch--Kato logarithm; the
    signed family is restricted to odd $p$ with $a_p=0$; the real readouts
    of $p$-adic data are stated as supplied inputs.
  \item The fifth factor of the five-column tensor is the orientation
    datum, not the assembled value, and the scalar comparison is stated
    for an orientation-normalized readout.
  \item The finite-shadow witness uses even $\Sha$ valuations; the height
    differential is stated with its off-diagonal factor $2$, and the
    heights of $389a1$ are recomputed for an explicit basis; the
    rank-one Heegner identity includes the index of the Heegner point;
    the rank-two calibration for $389a1$ no longer assumes
    $\#\Sha=1$; the $\kapr$ normalization states its nonzero hypotheses.
  \item Five small-prime supersingular rows of the support table are
    identified as outside the cited signed theory.
  \item Statements proved by Lean only in a model version are labelled
    as such; the literature remark is restated without an audit claim;
    the text is rewritten throughout for readability.
  \end{itemize}
\end{description}
